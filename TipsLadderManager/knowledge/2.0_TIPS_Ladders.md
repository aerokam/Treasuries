## TIPS Ladder Terminology

A [TIPS Ladder](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#tips-ladder) is a [Bond Ladder](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bond-ladder) built from TIPS, its targets and amounts stated in real terms, so [DARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#dara) takes the place of [DAA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#daa), and [ARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ara) that of [AA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#aa) ([1.0 §Bond Ladder Concepts](./1.0_Bond_Ladders.md)).

Also used throughout: [Funded Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#funded-year) and its synonym [Rung](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#rung), [Maturity Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#maturity-year), [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), and [Available Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#available-cash).

<a id="maturity-selection"></a>
## Maturity Selection Within a Funded Year

A [Funded Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#funded-year) holds one or more TIPS. [DARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#dara) is an annual target, and the P+I need of the year is split across the TIPS the year holds ([Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split](#multi-tips-funded-year-split)). The candidates are the [Outstanding TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#outstanding-tips) of the year: an auctioned TIPS that is not yet issued is a candidate in no mode and under no policy. In Build, a TIPS maturing no more than `EXCLUDE_MATURING_WITHIN_MONTHS` months after the [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values)) is a candidate under no policy and is not offered in the picker. Rebalance excludes none.

The Maturity preference selects the TIPS of every funded year from the TIPS Market Data alone, with no holdings involved. Build and Rebalance both select through `selectLadderBonds` ([4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)). The choices are:

- **Last to mature** (`last`, default): the latest-maturing TIPS of the year.
- **First to mature** (`first`): the earliest-maturing TIPS of the year.
- **Semiannual (Jan + Jul)** (`semiannual`): the January TIPS and the July TIPS, January first. A year with only one of the two has that one TIPS. A year with neither has its latest-maturing TIPS.
- **All maturity months** (`all`): one TIPS per distinct maturity month of the year, earliest first.
- **Select maturities…** (`select`): the TIPS picked for each year ([Picking Maturities for a Funded Year](#picking-maturities)).

**Two issues in one month.** In some years a maturity month has two TIPS with different coupons (on 2026-10-16: January 2025 through 2029 and April 2028 and 2029). Wherever a policy selects one TIPS for a month, including **All maturity months**, the Coupon preference selects it: **Higher** (`higher`, default) or **Lower** (`lower`) coupon. When the coupons are equal, the lower CUSIP is selected. Under **Last to mature** the tie-break is not reached, because the latest maturity month of every year has one issue (2026-10-16), and Build does not show the Coupon preference field for that choice.

- Build shows the Coupon preference field for the choices other than Last to mature and Select maturities.
- Rebalance has no Coupon preference field and selects **Higher** in every year.
- The Coupon preference does not apply to a year with picked TIPS: the picked TIPS are bought.

**Shared with Rebalance.** The Maturity preference is a control in both modes. The target side of Rebalance reads the same `yearTipsListMap` that Build reads. Which candidate of a year a Rebalance buys or sells is decided by the [Within-Year Allocation Policy](#within-year-allocation-policy).

<a id="picking-maturities"></a>
### Picking Maturities for a Funded Year

**Select maturities** is a Maturity preference choice. It replaces the other choices and opens a picker with one column per calendar month, a row per year and a checkbox per TIPS. Each column has All and None, and Low and High for a column where some year has two issues (the checked TIPS of each year is then the lower or the higher coupon).

- **Seeding.** In Build the checkboxes show the TIPS of the last Build. In Rebalance they show the TIPS held for the year. Unchecking a held TIPS in Rebalance is a sale of the whole holding, whatever the DARA of the year: every held TIPS of a year that the picks leave out is sold in full, and the picked TIPS are sized to the P+I need of the year ([3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md#target-cusip-resolution)).
- **Range.** While at least one TIPS is picked, the first year of the ladder is the earliest year with a pick and the last year is the latest year with a pick, in Build and in Rebalance. The First Year and Last Year fields are disabled and show that range. With no pick yet, both fields keep the values they held before the choice.
- **Years without a pick.** In Build and in Rebalance, the DARA of a year in range with no pick is 0, and the per-year DARA fields show the 0. The year is a rung that holds no TIPS, and it is classified as a gap, bracket or cover year as for any other year. This is the precedence a typed 0 DARA has.
- **Brackets.** In Build, the bracket role of a bracket year stays with its latest-maturing picked TIPS, so a pick can change the lower bracket duration. In Rebalance, the Excess TIPS of a bracket year stay in the TIPS that hold them, at the duration-matched quantity, whether or not that TIPS is picked. A held TIPS of a bracket year that the picks leave out has a funded contribution of 0: that funded contribution is sold and the picked TIPS carry the funded need of the year ([3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md#target-cusip-resolution)). Holding a TIPS only as Excess TIPS while another TIPS funds the year is allowed.
- **Changing the range.** Choosing a named Maturity preference enables the First Year and Last Year fields. The picks are kept, so choosing Select maturities again restores them. The 0 DARA given to years without a pick is removed when a named choice is made.
- **Allocation weight.** Each picked TIPS has an allocation weight ([Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split](#multi-tips-funded-year-split)). The picker writes equal weights.
- **Persistence.** The picks are not written to the Holdings Export or to the `#params` row ([4.2](./4.2_Import_Holdings_And_DARA_Plan.md)), so an import loses them.

`_effectiveYearRange` (`index.html`) is the one source of the range for the picker, the per-year DARA panel and the Build run. Rebalance passes the picked range as its first and last year only when at least one TIPS is picked.

<a id="multi-tips-funded-year-split"></a>
### Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split

For a funded year holding N ≥ 1 TIPS, ordered by maturity:

```
need    = yearDARA − incomingLMI − creditedInterest − partialCredit
share_i = need × w_i / Σw
for each TIPS i except the latest:
  qty_i = MAX(0, ROUND(share_i / piPerBond_i))
for the latest TIPS:
  qty_last = MAX(0, ROUND((need − Σ P+I delivered by the other TIPS) / piPerBond_last))
```

- `need` is the P+I need of the year after the income credited to it ([Algorithm](#algorithm-longest-to-shortest-sweep)).
- With equal weights, `share = need / N`. A weight changes the shares and not the rule.
- `piPerBond_i` is the [P+I per TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pi-per-tips) of TIPS i, so each TIPS is sized from its coupon and maturity.
- The latest-maturing TIPS takes the rounding remainder, so the P+I delivered by the year lands on `need`.
- N = 1 gives `qty = MAX(0, ROUND(need / piPerBond))`.
- The TIPS of one year never feed each other's LMI, because they mature in the same year.
- The year's funded quantity and annual interest (LMI of the shorter years) are the sums over its TIPS.

Implemented by `sizeYearRungs` and `aggregateRungs` in `ladder-core.js` ([4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)).

<a id="within-year-allocation-policy"></a>
### Within-Year Allocation Policy

The Maturity preference and the picks decide which TIPS are candidates for a funded year. The [Within-Year Allocation Policy](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#within-year-allocation-policy) decides which candidate a Rebalance trades when more than one is in play: several maturity months, several picked TIPS, or holdings that differ from the TIPS the Maturity preference selects today. Build splits its need evenly across all candidates ([Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split](#multi-tips-funded-year-split)).

**The policy orders the candidates and does not create trades.** A year whose real P+I need ([3.0 §Named Quantities & Formulas](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)) is unchanged has no trade under any policy. A year sells only when its total falls and buys only when it rises ([3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)).

The three ladder-wide policies each order the candidates and the held TIPS of a year, highest buy and hold priority first (`rankForYear`, `allocation-policy.js`, [4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)):

- **Maturity order** (`maturity`): by maturity date. **Last to mature** puts the latest-maturing TIPS first and **First to mature** the earliest-maturing. The other Maturity preferences use the latest-first order.
- **SA-Yield order** (`saYield`): by [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), highest first, so the lowest sells first. A TIPS with no SA Yield ranks after every TIPS that has one, and TIPS that have none are ordered as Maturity order orders them.
- **Equal split** (`equal`, default): the value of each TIPS (quantity × P+I per TIPS) is levelled (`levelValues`, `allocation-policy.js`).
  - Growth is added to the TIPS with the lowest value, and shrinkage is taken from the TIPS with the highest value.
  - TIPS whose values become equal are levelled together, so a trade does not move past equal values.
  - A candidate not held has value 0, so it takes the first purchase.

**Maturity order is fixed under Last to mature and First to mature.** Both choices fix one maturity direction for every year, which is the Maturity order policy. The Allocation policy control is then disabled and set to Maturity order. For **Semiannual**, **All maturity months** and **Select maturities** the control is enabled and its default is Equal split ([6.0 §Row 2](./6.0_UI_Layout.md)).

**Year override.** An order set for one year in the priority-order picker (**Set priority order…** in the Allocation policy control, [6.0 §Priority-Order Picker](./6.0_UI_Layout.md)) replaces the policy for that year. Choosing a named policy clears every year override.

**Scope.** Bracket, gap and cover Excess TIPS are not ordered by this policy: their identification ([Duration Matching (Brackets)](#duration-matching-brackets), [Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess)) does not call `rankForYear`, and Retained Bracket Excess is sold oldest first under any policy. The funded need of a bracket year that is also a funded year calls `rankForYear` with Maturity order only ([3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)). If the Excess TIPS held in the bracket year already covers the funded need, nothing moves.

<a id="gap-years-section"></a>
## Gap Years

The [Gap Years](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#gap-years) have no [Outstanding TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#outstanding-tips). [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) sizes a [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips) for each, and the duration match sets the [Excess TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#excess-tips) that the [Bracket Years](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-year) hold ([Duration Matching (Brackets)](#duration-matching-brackets)). Treasury issues the missing 10-year TIPS in later years, and a year with an issued TIPS is no longer a gap year and funds itself. A maturity year with no TIPS that is not a gap year, such as a year before the first year that has a TIPS, has no rung. The ladder starts at the first year that has a TIPS.

<a id="synthetic-tips-for-gap-years"></a>
**Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years.** A Synthetic TIPS is modeled as issued on the settlement date, so it is comparable to the TIPS on either side of it. No inflation is projected.

- **Maturity**: January 15 of the gap year. 10-year TIPS mature in January and July, and the January TIPS is the first to reach that year. The Synthetic TIPS of a Future 30Y year matures on February 15 ([Future 30Y Rungs](#future-30y-rungs)).
- **Index Ratio**: 1.00000 ([Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips)).
- **Coupon**: the eighth of a percent at or below the interpolated yield, and at least 0.125%, the lowest rate of Table 2 of Appendix B of 31 CFR 356 ([31 CFR 356 Definitions and Formulas](../../knowledge/31_CFR_356_Definitions_And_Formulas.md)). The coupon is counted in whole eighths (`syntheticCoupon`, `gap-math.js`).
- **Price**: calculated from the yield and coupon of the Synthetic TIPS (`priceFromYield`, [4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)). The coupon is the eighth at or below the yield, so the price is at or just below par at a positive real yield, and exactly par only at a yield that is a whole eighth. The price is above par when a negative real yield meets the 0.125% minimum coupon: at a real yield of −1% about 11 points above par. A price of 100 would bias the Gap Total Cost in one direction in ordinary conditions and strongly in that case.

<a id="gap-parameter-calculation"></a>
**Gap Parameter Calculation.** For the gap years, longest to shortest (`gapParamsCore`, `gap-math.js`):

- Find the anchors:
  - `anchorBefore`: the latest-maturing TIPS maturing before the first gap year of the ladder.
  - `anchorAfter`: the earliest-maturing February TIPS maturing after the last gap year.
- For each gap year:
  - Interpolate the yield linearly in maturity date between the anchors.
  - Set the coupon from the interpolated yield, as above.
  - Calculate the [Duration](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#duration) of the Synthetic TIPS as a modified duration.
  - Calculate the P+I per Synthetic TIPS: `1,000 × (1 + coupon / 2)`, one coupon in the year of maturity, in dollars at Index Ratio 1.00000.
  - Calculate the quantity: `qty = MAX(0, ROUND((yearDARA − runningSynLMI − actualTIPSLMI − pliCredit) / P+I))`, where `yearDARA` is the DARA of the year.
    - `runningSynLMI` is the annual interest of the Synthetic TIPS of the longer gap years already sized.
    - `actualTIPSLMI` is the annual interest of the TIPS of the funded years after the gap year. When Pre-Ladder Interest or Available Cash zeroes a funded year, that year contributes none.
    - `pliCredit` is the credit the gap year receives in the [Credit Pass](#credit-pass), and 0 without a credit.
  - Add the annual interest of the Synthetic TIPS, `qty × 1,000 × coupon`, to `runningSynLMI`.
  - Calculate the cost: `qty × Cost per TIPS`, where [Cost per TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cost-per-tips) is `(price / 100) × Index Ratio × 1,000` with Index Ratio 1.00000. A Synthetic TIPS issued on the settlement date has no accrued interest, so the clean price is the whole cost.
- The sum of the costs is the **Gap Total Cost**, which sizes the Excess TIPS ([Excess Quantities](#excess-quantities)). The average duration is described in [Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted](#average-gap-year-duration-is-cost-weighted).

<a id="gap-year-coverage-model"></a>
## Gap Year Coverage Model

The coverage of the gap years has two equivalent descriptions.

- **Synthetic ladder.** Every year of the ladder, gap years included, is a rung. Each gap year receives a Synthetic TIPS sized to deliver the DARA of the year, as for a funded year. The annual interest of each Synthetic TIPS is LMI of the shorter years.
- **Excess TIPS.** No TIPS exists for a gap year. Treasury issues each in January, ten years before its maturity year, so the model converts each Synthetic TIPS into a [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips) on its issue date ([Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#gap-year-simulated-outstanding-tips)). Until a Synthetic TIPS is converted, the [Excess TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#excess-tips) at the lower and upper bracket years carry its cost, split by [Bracket Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-weight), and each conversion sells the Excess TIPS that the unconverted Synthetic TIPS no longer need. The last conversion falls before the first gap year, so the Excess TIPS pays no interest in a gap year. Its interest is credited to the funded years in which it is paid ([Interest credited to a funded year](#gap-interest-credited)).

[LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi) is annual coupon interest, and never principal. P+I is applied to the funded year that holds the TIPS.

<a id="settlement-year-lmi"></a>
**Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only.** The funded year that contains the settlement date is already partway through its calendar year, so a coupon already paid is not forthcoming cash for that year. For that year only, the coupon of each contributing holding is:

```
remainingCoupons × couponPerPeriod × principalPerBond
remainingCoupons = remainingCouponPaymentsThisYear(maturity, settlementDate, bondHolidays, tradeDate)
couponPerPeriod  = coupon / 2
principalPerBond = 1,000 × paymentIndexRatio
```

- `remainingCouponPaymentsThisYear` counts the coupon dates (0, 1 or 2) of the calendar year of the settlement date that are not yet paid on the trade date.
- A coupon is unpaid when its payment date is later than the trade date. A coupon dated on a weekend or a bond holiday is paid on the next bond trading day (`actualPaymentDate`, `shared/src/settlement.js`; [5.0 §Cash Flow Calendar](./5.0_UI_Schema.md)), and that payment date is compared.
- The comparison uses the trade date, which is today, and not the settlement date. The settlement date is the trade date plus one bond trading day, and prices a trade placed today. A coupon paid between today and the settlement date is not yet in hand today. For a trade placed Friday August 14, a coupon dated Saturday August 15 is paid Monday August 17, and a comparison against the settlement date would count it as paid.
- The trade date defaults to the settlement date when a caller passes none (tests). Build and Rebalance pass the trade date of the page (`_tradeDateStr`), which is the date of the Cash Flow Calendar, so the ladder and the calendar agree on which coupons have paid.
- Every other funded year, every gap year and every bracket year is entirely in the future and keeps the full annual coupon.
- Build sizing (`sizeLadder`), the Before and After Amounts of Rebalance (`runRebalance`) and the Before-state rebuild use the same rule.
- Because coupons already paid stop counting, the incoming LMI of the settlement year falls with each coupon date that passes, and the year needs more principal to reach the same DARA. A Rebalance in February can buy into the current year when the same portfolio priced in the previous December did not.

### Maturity Proceeds Are Spent

A rung's principal is applied toward its funded year's Amount. After a holding matures it is absent from the market data and from the portfolio, and its principal is not carried forward as funds. The Net Cash of a Rebalance run shortly after a maturity therefore reports a purchase against money that has already been applied.

A holder who has not applied it enters [Available Cash](#available-cash). No other concept covers maturity proceeds, accumulated coupons or a distribution held back, because each is cash on hand that will be applied toward a funded year's Amount.

<a id="available-cash"></a>
### Available Cash

[Available Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#available-cash) (`availableCash`, dollars, default 0) is cash on hand that the holder applies toward the [ARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ara) of the earliest [Funded Years](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#funded-year), so those rungs need fewer TIPS or none.

- **Meaning.** Entering a figure states that the holder applies that cash toward the Amounts and does not invest it in TIPS. Cash intended for the ladder is not entered, and the box is left empty.
- **Entered, not detected.** The balance does not show which of the two uses is meant. The figure is not validated against holdings or coupon history. A non-zero value is the opt-in.
- **Sources.** The calculation does not depend on the source: a maturity not applied in the year it matured, accumulated coupons, cash set aside for a required minimum distribution, or cash not yet deployed.
- **Mode.** Rebalance only. Build starts from cash and reports what the ladder costs, so the control is not shown in Build and a Build sizes at 0 Available Cash, including when an imported DARA plan carries a figure from Rebalance. The Build and Rebalance sides keep separate values.
- **Use.** The figure is a credit applied to the earliest funded years first ([Credit Pass](#credit-pass)). A rung funded entirely by the credit pays no coupon and adds nothing to the LMI of a shorter year.
- **Persistence.** The `#params` row carries `availableCash=<dollars>`: the Holdings Export always writes it, `0` included, and the DARA Plan File writes it when non-zero ([2.1](./2.1_Export.md)). The key `rmdCashOverride=<dollars>` of earlier files is read as `availableCash` and is not written.
- **Control.** An input in the ladder parameters beside **Pre-ladder int.** ([6.0 §Row 1](./6.0_UI_Layout.md)), because the figure applies to the whole ladder.

**Cash already received.** A ladder loaded in Rebalance has been held over part of the current year, and its cash flows over that part have been received. The settlement-year income is assumed to be applied toward the Amount of the year ([Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment](#settlement-year-coupon-treatment)), so, where the settlement year is a funded year (the [default First Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values) is not after it) and the file records a `refCpiDate`, the app calculates the received cash and fills the box with it. The box is marked amber while the figure is the app's. Typing a figure removes the mark and the entered figure stands, and from then on the app does not change it, including when the box is cleared. A file that states `availableCash` is not offered a figure. A file that records no `refCpiDate`, a broker positions export included, says nothing about how long the ladder has been held, so the app enters nothing: the box starts at 0 as a typed amount. When the first funded year is after the settlement year, the settlement-year payments are applied toward no funded year, so the box starts at 0 for every file, a file with a `refCpiDate` included, and the holder counts the payments toward the funded years through the chooser.

The received cash has two components, both valued at the Ref CPI of the payment date through the code path of the Cash Flow Calendar ([5.0 §Cash Flow Calendar](./5.0_UI_Schema.md)), so one payment has one value:

- **Coupons already paid**, on every holding, whatever the choice of [Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment](#settlement-year-coupon-treatment). That choice covers only the coupons still to arrive, those with a payment date after today, so the two never overlap.
- **Maturity proceeds.** A matured rung paid its inflation-adjusted principal with its final coupon, which is the Amount of that year arriving. A matured TIPS is absent from the market data, so the app resolves it from [TIPS reference data (S2)](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#s2): the maturity, coupon and dated date Ref CPI give the amount paid. A broker positions export does not list a matured bond and records no date, so it is not offered a figure and the holder types the matured principal in the amount. A Holdings Export lists the matured CUSIP and its quantity, so its offer includes the proceeds.

**Which payments are counted.** The figure counts the payments of the settlement year after a starting payment date, which is exclusive: the payment made on that date is not counted and every later one is.

- The starting points offered are payment dates of this portfolio, since money arrives on no other day. Every settlement-year payment is one choice, and each payment date on or before today on which the portfolio was paid is another. The list comes from the payment schedules of the holdings, and each choice shows the amount it produces.
- The comparison uses the scheduled payment date, and not the shifted one. A coupon scheduled for July 15 is dropped by "after July 15" whether it was paid on the 15th or on the 17th.
- A file with a `refCpiDate` sets the starting point to the last payment date on or before that date, since a payment is in the account on its payment date. A date in an earlier year counts every settlement-year payment. The mapped choice is the earliest in the list, because the ladder was not held before it was saved. A ladder saved today produces 0 and the box stays empty, which gives zero trades on a build, export, import and rebalance on one day.
- A file with no `refCpiDate`, a broker positions export included, states no date, so the app has no basis for a figure and enters nothing: the box starts at 0 as a typed amount, and no payment is counted. The holder picks a starting point in the chooser or types a figure. This choice is separate from the Ref CPI date of the file ([3.0 §DARA Reference Date](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)): it sets which payments the figure includes and restates no amount.

**Control.** Clicking the Available Cash box opens a chooser. It lists every starting point with its amount and, as its last entry, Amount. Typing in the box selects Amount and clears the payment choices, with no click on the Amount button. Choosing a starting point asks for the app's figure and replaces a typed amount.

**Help.** The Available Cash help opens with two paragraphs: the cash available to apply toward the annual real amount of the earliest funded year or years, and that cash intended for reinvestment in the ladder is not entered and the box is left empty. One section, "Payments received this year", follows. It says that clicking the box counts the payments after a chosen payment date, or the holder types an amount; and that a Holdings file this app saved is entered for the holder and marked amber, and any other file starts at 0. A paragraph states that when the first funded year is after the settlement year the box starts at 0 for every file, and the chooser counts the settlement-year payments toward the funded years. A final paragraph says that a broker positions export does not include TIPS that have matured, so the holder includes in Available Cash the proceeds from these that apply toward funded year amounts.

<a id="pre-ladder-interest"></a>
### Pre-Ladder Interest

[Pre-Ladder Interest](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pre-ladder-interest) (`preLadderInterest`, **Pre-ladder int.**, default off) credits the interest received before the first funded year toward the earliest funded years. It applies in Build and in Rebalance. It is the sum of the following amounts, for each year from the settlement year to the year before the first funded year (`preLadderYears`):

- the annual interest of the preliminary sweep (`prelim`, [Algorithm](#algorithm-longest-to-shortest-sweep)), once per year
- the three amounts credited to that year for the Future 30Y years ([Interest credited to a funded year](#future-30y-interest-credited))
- the three amounts credited to that year for the gap years, from the estimate stage of `gapStage` described in [Credit Pass](#credit-pass)

<a id="credit-pass"></a>
**Credit Pass.** Available Cash and Pre-Ladder Interest are two inputs of one pass, in `sizeLadder` ([4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)). They are separate inputs because Pre-Ladder Interest comes from coupon history and applies only to a ladder that starts in a later year, while Available Cash is a figure the holder states and applies whenever it is non-zero.

- Set the amount to apply (`creditPool`) to `availableCash + preLadderPool`. Do nothing when it is 0.
- For each year in `rangeYears ∪ gapYears`, earliest first, for years not before the first year:
  - For a gap year:
    - `need = MAX(0, yearDARA − actualTIPSLMI)`, where `actualTIPSLMI` is the annual interest of the preliminary sweep for the years after it.
    - If the amount remaining covers `need`, the gap year receives `need` and the amount remaining falls by `need`. Otherwise the gap year receives the rest of the amount remaining and the pass ends.
  - For a funded year:
    - `need = yearDARA − incomingLMI − future30yCredit − gapCredit`, where `incomingLMI` is the full annual LMI, and for the settlement year the LMI of the remaining coupons ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi)), and the two credits are the amounts credited to the year for the Future 30Y years and the gap years.
    - If `need ≤ 0`, the year is zeroed and receives 0.
    - If the amount remaining covers `need`, the year is zeroed, receives `need`, and the amount remaining falls by `need`.
    - Otherwise the year receives the rest of the amount remaining and the pass ends. This is the partial-credit year, and every later year is unchanged.
  - Available Cash is spent first, being money in hand against coupons still to arrive. Each year records the credit from cash and the credit from Pre-Ladder Interest separately, so a drill can show where each dollar came from.
- A zeroed year funds nothing, so it contributes no interest to the LMI of a shorter year, and it is excluded from the `actualTIPSLMI` of the gap years.
- The credit shown for a zeroed year in its Amount is the amount that tops the year up to its DARA against the income actually credited (`fundedYearAmount`). The credit does not change a trade quantity.
- The gap stage is sized twice. The estimate has no pre-ladder credit for the gap years and sizes the Pre-Ladder Interest. The stage that the sweep and the Amounts read is sized again with the credit each gap year then receives. The difference does not reach an Amount, because `fundedYearAmount` reconciles a zeroed year against its DARA.

<a id="settlement-year-coupon-treatment"></a>
### Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment

Whether a coupon not yet paid this year is available when needed depends on what happens to it meanwhile: reinvested in the ladder, or held in a money market fund or a Treasury bill. The calendar does not show which, so the choice is stated. It applies only to the settlement year, the only funded year with a split between coupons already paid and coupons still to come.

**Count remaining coupons** (`rmdCouponMode`, default `all`):

- `all`: every remaining coupon of the year counts ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi) unchanged). The coupon cash is held in cash or short Treasuries until the target date.
- `last`: only the coupons paid on the latest remaining coupon date of the year count, over all bonds that contribute to the settlement-year LMI (`latestRemainingCouponDate`, `ladder-core.js`). A bond whose remaining coupon is earlier than that date is treated as reinvested. The date is for the whole set of bonds because a ladder holds bonds on different semiannual cycles, and by mid-year most bonds have one coupon left.
- `none`: no remaining coupon counts, and the settlement year is sized from principal and Available Cash alone. Every remaining coupon is reinvested.

- The latest date is calculated once per sizing pass over the bonds that pass draws its settlement-year LMI from: `sizeLadder` over the bonds of `rangeYears`, the Before state of `runRebalance` over the bonds held, and its After state over the target bonds of `selectLadderBonds`. Every call of `rmdCappedRemainingCoupons` in the pass uses that date.
- The choice sets the settlement-year income in the same places as the coupon LMI: `incomingLMI` and the credited interest of the Excess TIPS and Cover Excess use the counts of the choice. The resulting need is what the Available Cash credit is charged against. It applies in `sizeLadder`, in the Before-state ARA recovery and the After-state rebuild of `runRebalance`, and in the displayed Amount of `fundedYearAmount`.
- The defaults (0 Available Cash and `all`) give the settlement-year LMI of [Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi).
- A holder who takes a distribution early in the year uses the same two inputs. Available Cash is what is already on hand, and `none` assigns every unpaid coupon to the rung of the next year.
- **Mode.** Rebalance only. The choice divides the settlement year into coupons already received and coupons still to come, and a Build has no first half: every coupon from the build date is its income. A Build sizes at `all` whatever a loaded file or the Rebalance side holds, and does not show the control. The Rebalance side keeps a separate value, as for Available Cash.
- **Control.** A link on the settlement year's group header row in Rebalance ([5.0 §Funded Year Group Header Row](./5.0_UI_Schema.md)) opens a popover with this setting. The popover names the coupons already received, which are counted in Available Cash and not here, and the number of payment dates left in the year. With one date left `all` and `last` count the same coupon, and with none left all three count nothing. The figures are the live ones of the loaded portfolio.
- **Persistence.** The `#params` row carries `rmdCouponMode=<all|last|none>`: the Holdings Export always writes it and the DARA Plan File writes it when not `all` ([2.1](./2.1_Export.md)). A Rebalance restores a non-default value from a file, and a Build ignores it.

<a id="gap-year-simulated-outstanding-tips"></a>
## Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS

The gap years have no outstanding TIPS yet. In [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) each is a [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips). Treasury auctions a 10-year TIPS every January and July and issues it on the last trading day of that month. A Synthetic TIPS of a gap year matures in January ([Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years](#synthetic-tips-for-gap-years)), so each gap year gets an outstanding TIPS in turn. The model follows that, as it does for the Future 30Y years ([Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#future-30y-simulated-outstanding-tips)). On the issue date the Synthetic TIPS of a gap year becomes a [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips), and the [Excess TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#excess-tips) is sold down as the conversions proceed. [Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2) then credits each funded year with the interest that the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS and the Excess TIPS pay in that year.

### Issue dates

A gap year's TIPS is issued on the last trading day of January, 10 years before its maturity year. A TIPS maturing in January 2039 is issued on the last trading day of January 2029. The first gap year of the ladder is converted on the first issue date after settlement. The conversions are taken in order, earliest issue date first (`gapIssueDate`, `ladder-core.js`).

### What happens on each issue date

Each conversion is a rebalance, with everything measured on the issue date. The Synthetic TIPS keep the yield and coupon that Pass 1 gave them, and each bracket TIPS keeps its yield and coupon.

1. The Synthetic TIPS not yet issued are the gap years later than the one being issued. Their average duration is cost-weighted ([Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted](#average-gap-year-duration-is-cost-weighted)).
2. Both bracket TIPS are older than at the last conversion, so their modified durations are shorter. The [Bracket Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-weight) of each is solved again from the two durations, as in [2-Bracket Weights](#2-bracket-weights), and held within 0 to 1.
3. The Excess TIPS each bracket holds after the conversion is the total cost of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued, times its Bracket Weight, divided by its cost per TIPS, rounded to a whole number of TIPS.
4. Excess TIPS above that quantity is sold, and a bracket that holds less than that quantity buys the difference. The Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS is bought at the quantity that Pass 1 sized. The [Net Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#net-cash) is the proceeds of the Excess TIPS sold less the cost of the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS, and is positive or negative.

After the last conversion no Synthetic TIPS are left, so the Excess TIPS is 0. In a Rebalance, [Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess) decides which lower bracket Excess TIPS is sold on an issue date, and is never bought.

<a id="gap-interest-credited"></a>
### Interest credited to a funded year

Each funded year before the first gap year is credited, in its [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), with three amounts, alongside the interest of every TIPS held that matures later. When the ladder also has Future 30Y years, these are in addition to the three amounts of [Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#future-30y-interest-credited).

- **From the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS.** A Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS is issued after its January 15 coupon date, so it pays one coupon in its issue year (July 15) and two in each later year. Each coupon is half the annual coupon, which is the quantity × 1,000 × the synthetic coupon. A funded year is credited with every Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS issued by that year.
- **From the Excess TIPS.** Each bracket TIPS pays on two coupon dates a year, and the quantity on a coupon date is the quantity held on that date. A coupon date earlier in the year than the issue date is paid on the quantity held before that year's conversion, and a later one on the quantity held after it. A bracket TIPS that matures in January or July pays its January 15 coupon on the quantity before the conversion and its July 15 coupon on the quantity after it. One that matures in February or August pays both on the quantity after it. A year with no conversion pays both coupons on one quantity. Before the first conversion the quantity is the one that Pass 1 sized. A coupon on or after the maturity date is not paid, because the Excess TIPS is sold before it matures.
- **From the Net Cash.** The Net Cash of an issue date is cash delivered in the year of that date, and is credited to the funded year of that year.

A coupon counts toward the funded year in which it is paid, whatever the month, for an actual coupon and for a simulated one. The number of coupons differs from year to year, so these amounts are worked out for each funded year directly and are not carried in the running LMI that the sweep passes to the shorter years. In the settlement year only a coupon not yet paid counts ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi)), filtered by the coupon-counting choice ([Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment](#settlement-year-coupon-treatment)). The Net Cash is not a coupon and is not filtered.

From the year after the last conversion the Excess TIPS is 0 and every Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS pays two coupons a year. That total is the figure that Pass 1 uses as the LMI of the Synthetic TIPS.

**Pre-Ladder Interest.** [Pre-Ladder Interest](#pre-ladder-interest) includes all three amounts for the years before the first funded year.

<a id="future-30y-rungs"></a>
## Future 30Y Rungs

**Future 30Y Rungs** are the years of the ladder after the maturity year of the longest-dated issued TIPS. They arise when the last year of the ladder runs past that maturity year, and reach at most the Maximum Future 30Y Rungs beyond it ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values), [Limit on the ladder's last year](#limit-on-the-ladders-last-year)).

**Hypothetical TIPS for Future 30Y Rungs.** The Synthetic TIPS are processed longest to shortest (`future30yParamsCore`, `gap-math.js`), with the longest-dated issued TIPS as the anchor of a flat curve:

- **Anchor yield**: the yield of the longest-dated issued TIPS, the most recently issued 30-year TIPS. The yield is the same for every Future 30Y year.
- **Synthetic coupon**: the eighth of a percent at or below the anchor yield, and at least 0.125%, as for the gap years ([Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years](#synthetic-tips-for-gap-years)). The coupon of the longest-dated issued TIPS is not used.
- **Maturity**: February 15 of the Future 30Y year, because 30-year TIPS are issued in February.
- **Index Ratio**: 1.00000, as for the gap years.
- **Price**: calculated from the anchor yield and the synthetic coupon, with the same result as for the gap years: at or just below par at a positive real yield, and above par when a negative real yield meets the 0.125% minimum coupon.
- **Duration**: modified duration, from the synthetic coupon and the anchor yield.
- **Quantity**: `qty = MAX(0, ROUND((yearDARA − runningLMI) / P+I))`, where `runningLMI` is the annual interest of the Synthetic TIPS of the longer Future 30Y years already sized. No TIPS exists after these years, so no actual TIPS and no Pre-Ladder Interest enters the quantity.
- **Cost**: `qty × Cost per TIPS` at Index Ratio 1.00000, as for the gap years.

**Recovering the last funded year on import.** A Future 30Y year is not held as TIPS. The Cover Excess at the two covers represents it, and the Holdings Export and the tipsladder.com Format state it in their excess quantity ([Holdings File](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#holdings-file)). `inferLastYearFromHoldings` (`rebalance-lib.js`) recovers the last funded year from that quantity:

- The cost-weighted split between the upper cover and the lower cover is a duration match. It does not depend on DARA and it moves with the number of Future 30Y years.
- For each candidate last year, calculate the upper cover weight of the build for that number of years (`future30yParamsCore` and `bracketWeights`), and select the year whose weight is nearest the observed upper cover weight.
- A holdings file without an excess quantity at the covers, or with an excess quantity of 0, gives no recovered year, and the ladder ends at the last issued TIPS.
- A cover whose maturity year is at most one year after the last gap year gives no recovered year, because the excess column cannot tell it from bracket excess.
- The holder can override the recovered year.

The first funded year is recovered from the gap years (`inferFirstYearFromHoldings`) with an excess-to-DARA ratio: the total P+I of the Excess TIPS is about the number of gap years × the DARA. The ratio does not transfer to Future 30Y years, because the upper cover is a deep-discount TIPS and a long run of Future 30Y years has a large LMI cascade.

<a id="future-30y-simulated-outstanding-tips"></a>
## Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS

The Future 30Y years have no outstanding TIPS yet. In [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) each is a [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips). Treasury auctions a 30-year TIPS every February and issues it on the last trading day of that month, so each Future 30Y year gets an outstanding TIPS in turn. The model follows that. On the issue date the Synthetic TIPS of a Future 30Y year becomes a [Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-30y-tips), and the [Cover Excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cover-excess) is sold down as the conversions proceed. [Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2) then credits each funded year with the interest that the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS and the Cover Excess pay in that year.

### Issue dates

A Future 30Y year's TIPS is issued on the last trading day of February, 30 years before its maturity year. A TIPS maturing in February 2066 is issued on the last trading day of February 2036. The first Future 30Y year is the year after the longest issued maturity year, and its issue date is the first February after settlement. The conversions are taken in order, earliest issue date first (`future30yIssueDate`, `ladder-core.js`).

### What happens on each issue date

Each conversion is a rebalance, with everything measured on the issue date. The Synthetic TIPS keep the yield and coupon that Pass 1 gave them, and each cover keeps its yield and coupon.

1. The Synthetic TIPS not yet issued are the Future 30Y years later than the one being issued. Their average duration is cost-weighted ([Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted](#average-gap-year-duration-is-cost-weighted)).
2. Both covers are older than at the last conversion, so their modified durations are shorter. The [Cover Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cover-weight) of each cover is solved again from the two durations, as in [2-Bracket Weights](#2-bracket-weights), and held within 0 to 1.
3. The Cover Excess of each cover is set to the total cost of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued, times its Cover Weight, divided by its cost per TIPS, rounded to a whole number of TIPS.
4. The Cover Excess that falls is sold, the Cover Excess that rises is bought, and the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS is bought at the quantity that Pass 1 sized. The [Net Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#net-cash) is positive, apart from the rounding of quantities to whole TIPS: the Cover Excess is bought at a discount and rises in price toward par between issue dates, while the Synthetic TIPS are priced near par. It is the return of the Cover Excess, which takes the place of the coupons that the Future 30Y TIPS would pay if all had been issued today ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).

After the last conversion no Synthetic TIPS are left, so the Cover Excess is 0.

<a id="future-30y-interest-credited"></a>
### Interest credited to a funded year

Each funded year before the first Future 30Y year is credited, in its [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), with three amounts, alongside the interest of every TIPS held that matures later.

- **From the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS.** A Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS is issued after its February 15 coupon date, so it pays one coupon in its issue year (August 15) and two in each later year. Each coupon is half the annual coupon, which is the quantity × 1,000 × the synthetic coupon. A funded year is credited with every Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS issued by that year. The first funded year receives one coupon from the first, the second funded year receives two from the first and one from the next, and so on.
- **From the Cover Excess.** Each cover pays on February 15 and August 15. The February coupon is on the quantity held before the conversion of that year, and the August coupon on the quantity held after it. A year with no conversion pays both coupons on one quantity. Before the first conversion the quantity is the one that Pass 1 sized.
- **From the Net Cash.** The Net Cash of an issue date is cash delivered in the year of that date, and is credited to the funded year of that year.

A coupon counts toward the funded year in which it is paid, whatever the month, for an actual coupon and for a simulated one. The number of coupons differs from year to year, so these amounts are worked out for each funded year directly and are not carried in the running LMI that the sweep passes to the shorter years. In the settlement year only a coupon not yet paid counts ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi)).

From the year after the last conversion the Cover Excess is 0 and every Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS pays two coupons a year. That total is the figure that Pass 1 uses as the LMI of the Synthetic TIPS. While the yields of the covers are close to the anchor yield, the three amounts total about the full annual coupons of every Future 30Y TIPS over the years of the rebalances, as if all had been issued today, short by the accrued interest of the first coupon at settlement ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).

**Pre-Ladder Interest.** [Pre-Ladder Interest](#pre-ladder-interest) includes all three amounts for the years before the first funded year.

<a id="limit-on-the-ladders-last-year"></a>
### Limit on the ladder's last year

The last year of the ladder is at most the longest issued maturity year plus the Maximum Future 30Y Rungs ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values)). No outstanding TIPS matures after the Future 30Y years, so each cover gets shorter with every conversion, and the TIPS with the longest duration is one with a very low coupon. The Maximum Future 30Y Rungs is the largest number of Future 30Y years for which, on every issue date, the average duration of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued lies between the durations of the two covers (`future30yLimit`, `ladder-core.js`). With more years, the Cover Weight is held at 0 or 1 on some issue date and the duration match is not reached, so a ladder with a later last year is not offered.

The Maximum Future 30Y Rungs is derived on each run and has no margin. A fall in yields lowers it, so a ladder built to the maximum may not reach its duration match after yields fall. Future 30Y years are an experimental feature, and the duration match is not reliable when yields fall a lot.

<a id="duration-matching-brackets"></a>
## Duration Matching (Brackets)

The gap years and the Future 30Y years are covered by additional holdings in a **bracket pair** (**cover pair** for the Future 30Y years). The excess holdings change in value as the gap years do when rates move. The duration drills are specified in [5.0 §Duration Disclosure Standards](./5.0_UI_Schema.md).

**Modified duration throughout.** Every duration in this section is a modified duration (`calculateMDuration`, [TIPS Basics §Duration](../../knowledge/TIPS_Basics.md)): the duration of the lower bracket and of the upper bracket, the duration of each Synthetic TIPS, and the average duration of the gap years or of the Future 30Y years. Modified duration is the sensitivity of price to yield, which is what makes the value of the bracket pair move with the value of the gap years under a rate shift.

<a id="average-gap-year-duration-is-cost-weighted"></a>
### Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted

The average duration of the gap years is the cost-weighted mean of the durations of their Synthetic TIPS. The average duration of the Future 30Y years is calculated the same way.

```
avgDuration = Σ(rungCost × rungDuration) / Σ rungCost
rungCost        = rung.qty × costPerBond
```

- The weight is the cost because the DARA can differ from year to year, and a rung with more cost moves the average toward its duration.
- When `Σ rungCost = 0`, the average is the simple mean of the rung durations.

<a id="2-bracket-weights"></a>
### 2-Bracket Weights

```
lowerWeight × d_lower + upperWeight × d_upper = avgDuration
upperWeight = CLAMP((avgDuration − d_lower) / (d_upper − d_lower), 0, 1)
lowerWeight = 1 − upperWeight
```

- The weights are shares of the total cost covered, so neither is below 0 or above 1.
- When `avgDuration` is outside `[d_lower, d_upper]`, the whole weight is held at the nearer cover and the duration match is not reached. This arises for the Future 30Y years: a deep-discount cover with a near-zero coupon has a long duration for its maturity, so the average duration of the Future 30Y years can lie outside the span of the two cover durations.
- When the two durations differ by less than 0.0001, the weights are 0.5 and 0.5.
- Implemented by `bracketWeights` (`gap-math.js`, [4.0](./4.0_Computation_Modules.md)).

**The lower bracket.** When gap years are in the range of the ladder, the lower bracket is the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket): the latest-maturing outstanding TIPS with a yield that matures before the first gap year of the ladder, found in the TIPS Market Data. The latest-maturing TIPS is used, and not the latest January TIPS: a year before the gap years can hold a January TIPS and a July TIPS, and the later one gives the better duration match. An earlier maturity of that year, if held, is a funded-year holding and has no bracket role.

- When the first year of the ladder is inside the gap years, the lower bracket year is before the first year. It is added to `yearBondMap` and `rangeYears` before the sweep with a funded quantity of 0, as pure Excess TIPS.
- The upper bracket year (2040) is added the same way when it is not in the range.
- When the first year of the ladder is 2040 or later, the ladder has no gap years and no bracket logic.
- Duration matching uses the 2-bracket formula with this lower bracket.
- Rebalance uses this lower bracket whatever the holdings are. In **2-bracket** mode an older lower bracket maturity falls into the normal rebalance sweep and is rebuilt to the funded-year quantity only. In **Multi-bracket** mode its excess is retained ([Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess)).

<a id="retained-bracket-excess"></a>
### Retained Bracket Excess (Rebalance)

[Retained Bracket Excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#retained-bracket-excess) is lower bracket excess held in maturities older than the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket). It arises because a rebalance buys the active lower bracket of its time, and when a later maturity is issued the earlier one becomes retained. The number of retained maturities grows by one with each issuance before the gap years. The same rule applies to zero retained maturities and to five.

A retained maturity is older and so shorter than the active lower bracket. Priced at the active duration, it leaves the gap years under-matched. Each retained maturity therefore keeps its duration, and only the active lower bracket and the upper bracket are solved (`bracketWeightsN`, `gap-math.js`):

```
R   = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r) / totalCost                (retained share, frozen)
D_R = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r / totalCost) × d_r          (duration contribution of the retained maturities)

upperWeight  = ((dGap − D_R) − (1 − R) × d_activeLower) / (d_upper − d_activeLower)
activeWeight = (1 − R) − upperWeight
```

`dGap` is the average duration of the gap years and `totalCost` is the Gap Total Cost. The retained shares are inputs, and the duration match `Σ wᵣ·dᵣ + w_active·d_active + w_upper·d_upper = dGap` has two unknowns, `w_active` and `w_upper`.

**The active lower bracket's floor.** The active lower bracket is the only lower bracket that a rebalance buys ([Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket)), and it is not sold to make room for the duration match. Its floor is the share of the total cost of the excess that it holds now (`activeFloorWeight`). The retained excess is over-allocated when `activeWeight` is below the floor, and not only when it is below 0.

**Over-allocated** (`activeWeight` below the floor, so the lower brackets carry more than the gap years need):

- Sell the earliest retained maturity, and only as much as restoring the match requires: solve for the weight of that maturity that brings `activeWeight` to exactly the floor, hold every other retained maturity fixed, and limit the weight to `[0, held]`.
- If selling that maturity entirely is not enough, apply the same treatment to the next earliest retained maturity.
- Retained excess is never bought. Purchases are in the active lower bracket and the upper bracket, which absorb what the retained maturities no longer cover.

**Under-allocated** (`activeWeight` at or above the floor with all retained excess at its current quantity):

- Hold all retained excess at its current quantity, never increased.
- Buy the active lower bracket for the rest, never below its floor.

For each lower bracket `b`: `excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(targetCost_b / costPerBond_b)`.

**Which maturities are retained.** The maturities passed to `bracketWeightsN` are:

- one cross-year [Retained Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#retained-lower-bracket), from the Excess ARA pick, when it is a different TIPS from the active lower bracket (compared by CUSIP). The search covers maturity years from `LOWEST_LOWER_BRACKET_YEAR` ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values)) to the year before the first gap year, and
- every held TIPS that matures earlier than the active lower bracket in the maturity year of the active lower bracket ([Bracket Year TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-year-tips): a maturity year can hold a January TIPS and a July TIPS). Its excess is what it holds beyond its funded need, which is met from the earliest maturity first. An imported excess quantity states the split for each CUSIP ([3.0 §Bracket Identification Rules](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)).

More than one cross-year retained maturity is not identified from holdings. The active lower bracket is always resolved from the TIPS Market Data and never from holdings.

`runRebalance` reports the weight of every retained maturity it solved, cross-year and same-year, as `retainedLegs` (`{cusip, year, duration, weight}`, earliest maturity first), so that `Σ retainedLegs[i].weight × duration + newLowerWeight3 × newLowerDuration + upperWeight × upperDuration = dGap`, and a sum of the weights of 1, can be checked from the summary.

**Brackets control** (Rebalance only; **Multi-bracket** is the default):

- **2-bracket**: retained excess is sold outright and the bracket role consolidates into the active lower bracket. Those maturities fall into the normal rebalance sweep and are rebuilt to the funded-year quantity only.
- **Multi-bracket**: the priority rule above applies.
- The control sets the excess only. The funded-year holdings in those maturities are the same in both modes.

**Round-Trip Rounding Note.** The over-allocated partial-sell target is a dollar figure, and the holding is rounded to a whole number of TIPS (`excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(...)`). A rounded holding can be a few dollars short of the exact target. Exporting and importing the rounded portfolio can therefore show a small residual trade on the next rebalance: up to about one TIPS, bought into the active lower bracket, because retained excess is never bought. That TIPS changes the interest credited to every shorter funded year by a fraction of a TIPS, so a funded year whose unrounded quantity is near a half TIPS can round to the other whole number. Measured on live prices on 2026-10-15, the first rerun bought one TIPS into the active lower bracket and moved three funded years by one TIPS each (2027 −1, 2028 +2, 2029 −1; net funded quantity unchanged; cash −$992). The number of funded years near a half TIPS depends on the prices of the day, so the size of the first rerun is not fixed. A rerun of that output makes no trades. The residual does not occur in the under-allocated case, where retained excess is at its current whole-TIPS quantity, or when no retained maturity is held. The test suite asserts that the rerun has no trades within 3 further round trips and that cash stays within the cost of one TIPS (`tests/run.js`).

<a id="future-30y-cover-pair"></a>
### Future 30Y Cover Pair

When the last year of the ladder is after the longest issued maturity year, two outstanding TIPS duration-match the Synthetic TIPS of the Future 30Y years (`selectFuture30yCovers`, `ladder-core.js`):

- **Lower cover**: the outstanding TIPS with the longest modified duration at or below that of the Synthetic TIPS of the first Future 30Y year alone, measured at `LOWER_COVER_YIELD` ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values)). That is the lowest target that any ladder has, so the lower cover is at or below the target of every ladder. A fixed yield above the market yield keeps the choice from changing with the yield of the day ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).
- **Upper cover**: the outstanding TIPS with the longest modified duration, excluding the lower cover.

The covers are chosen for the longest duration and not the longest maturity. No outstanding TIPS matures after the Future 30Y years, so only a coupon far below current yields gives a high duration. Such a TIPS behaves close to a zero-coupon security, and its duration can exceed that of a later maturity. Which maturities are covers therefore changes with the market ([DD §5.1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#5.1-issuance-dependent-values)).

<a id="excess-quantities"></a>
### Excess Quantities

For each bracket `b`:

```
excessQty_b = ROUND(totalCost × weight_b / costPerBond_b)
```

`costPerBond_b` is the [Cost per TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cost-per-tips) of the bracket, `(price / 100) × Index Ratio × 1,000`, and `totalCost` is the Gap Total Cost (the Future 30Y Total Cost for a cover pair). The formula applies to the upper bracket (2040), and to the lower brackets when no Retained Bracket Excess is held. When lower bracket excess exists in maturities older than the active lower bracket, the priority rule of [Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess) replaces the weight formula across the lower brackets, and the upper bracket keeps this formula.

<a id="amount-of-a-gap-year-and-a-future-30y-year"></a>
### Amount of a Gap Year and a Future 30Y Year

The Amount of a gap year or a Future 30Y year is on the row of that year, as the Amount of a funded year is on its row, in Build and in Rebalance, before and after. It is what the TIPS and the [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi) of the year deliver in that year, and comes to about the DARA of the year, apart from the rounding of the quantity to a whole number of TIPS (`syntheticYearAmounts`, `ladder-core.js`).

- **Gap year**: the P+I of its [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips) plus the LMI that sized its quantity (the interest of the later gap years and the pre-ladder credit). By the year it matures all of the later gap years have been issued, so that interest is their full annual coupons, the figure that [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) uses as LMI. The Amount equals the Amount that Pass 1 gives its Synthetic TIPS.
- **Future 30Y year**: the P+I of its [Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-30y-tips) plus the interest of the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS of the later Future 30Y years. By the year it matures all of them have been issued, so that interest is their full annual coupons, the figure that Pass 1 uses as LMI. The Amount equals the Amount that Pass 1 gives its Synthetic TIPS.

The excess rows show no Amount. What matters on them is the cost of the excess, which duration matching sets, so their popups show how that cost is reached.

<a id="two-pass-walkthrough"></a>
## Two Passes

The gap and bracket rules above ([Gap Years](#gap-years-section), [Gap Year Coverage Model](#gap-year-coverage-model), [Duration Matching (Brackets)](#duration-matching-brackets)) are two passes over the ladder.

**[Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1): size the Synthetic TIPS, longest to shortest.** Synthetic TIPS stand in for the gap years ([Gap Parameter Calculation](#gap-parameter-calculation)). The quantity of each is its DARA less its incoming LMI, as for any funded year. The incoming LMI has two sources: the actual TIPS above the gap years (`actualTIPSLMI`), and the running total of the coupons of the Synthetic TIPS already sized for the longer gap years in the same pass (`runningSynLMI`). The output is the average duration of the gap years and the Gap Total Cost ([Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted](#average-gap-year-duration-is-cost-weighted)). The Excess TIPS pays no interest in a gap year, because the last conversion sells it ([Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#gap-year-simulated-outstanding-tips)), so the incoming LMI has no third source.

**Between the passes: duration-match the excess.** The average duration of the Synthetic TIPS from Pass 1 is the target that the Excess TIPS of the bracket years match. Solving the weight equations ([2-Bracket Weights](#2-bracket-weights), or `bracketWeightsN` of [Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess) when older retained maturities keep their durations) divides the Gap Total Cost among the bracket years so that the cost-weighted mean of their durations equals the average duration of the gap years. That sets the Excess TIPS quantity of each bracket year ([Excess Quantities](#excess-quantities)). The cover years of the Future 30Y years are matched the same way, against the Future 30Y years and their total cost ([Future 30Y Cover Pair](#future-30y-cover-pair)). The Gap Dur popup shows the Pass 1 sizing for each year with the live DARA and duration figures of the ladder, and the Cost popup on an excess row shows the weight solve and the quantity.

**[Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2): build the real ladder, longest to shortest.** With the Excess TIPS quantities known, the sweep of [Algorithm](#algorithm-longest-to-shortest-sweep) runs over the actual holdings. The Excess TIPS is sold before it matures, so it pays no interest in its maturity year ([Same-Maturity Excess Interest](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#same-maturity-excess-interest)). Its interest, with that of the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS and the Net Cash, is credited to the funded years before the first gap year ([Interest credited to a funded year](#gap-interest-credited)).

**Why a rebalance is needed later.** The synthetic coupon of Pass 1 is an interpolated yield, and the actual coupon of a gap year TIPS is not known until Treasury issues it. Each rebalance after a gap year TIPS is issued replaces its Synthetic TIPS with the actual TIPS and resizes the Excess TIPS against the remaining gap years.

The transition is not configured or scheduled. The gap years are derived from the market data on every run (`getGapYears`), so a year stops being a gap year when its TIPS is tradable, and the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket) advances to it by the same rule. On the first rebalance after that:

- The new maturity is bought: its funded-year rung and, in 2-bracket mode, its share of the lower bracket excess for the remaining gap years.
- The previous lower bracket is sold down. In 2-bracket mode its entire excess is sold, because the bracket role has moved. In Multi-bracket mode it is retained and trimmed only as the gap years shrink ([Retained Bracket Excess](#retained-bracket-excess)).
- The upper bracket is trimmed, because the gap years span one fewer year.
- Other quantities change a little: the actual coupon differs from the interpolated synthetic coupon and the curve has moved since the build.

A Rebalance run partway through a calendar year also buys into the current rung for another reason ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi)). Inflation adds nothing when the DARA is stated at one Ref CPI date ([3.0 §DARA Reference Date](./3.0_TIPS_Ladder_Rebalancing.md)).

<a id="algorithm-longest-to-shortest-sweep"></a>
## Algorithm (Longest-to-Shortest Sweep)

The sweep sizes the actual funded years, longest to shortest (`sizeLadder`, `ladder-core.js`):

- Set `runningLMI = 0` and `runningLMIRemaining = 0`.
- For each year Y, longest to shortest:
  - Calculate the incoming LMI:
    - for the settlement year, `runningLMIRemaining`: the annual interest of the bonds maturing after Y, each counted for its remaining coupons ([Settlement-year LMI](#settlement-year-lmi))
    - for every other year, `runningLMI`: the annual interest of the bonds maturing after Y, at the payment Index Ratio
  - Calculate the credited interest: the amounts credited to Y for the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS, the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS, the Excess TIPS, the Cover Excess and the Net Cash ([Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#gap-year-simulated-outstanding-tips), [Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS](#future-30y-simulated-outstanding-tips)). They are worked out for Y directly. The Excess TIPS and the Cover Excess are sold before they mature, so none pays interest in its maturity year.
  - Calculate the need:
    - `need = 0` for a zeroed year ([Credit Pass](#credit-pass)) and for a year outside the first and last year of the ladder
    - otherwise `need = MAX(0, yearDARA − incomingLMI − creditedInterest − partialCredit)`, where `partialCredit` is the credit of the partial-credit year and 0 for every other year
  - Calculate the funded quantity from `need` ([Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split](#multi-tips-funded-year-split)).
  - Add the annual interest of the funded TIPS to `runningLMI`, and the interest of their remaining coupons to `runningLMIRemaining`.
- A gap year adds nothing to `runningLMI`: no bonds are held in it, and the Excess TIPS interest is credited to the funded years directly.
