## TIPS Ladder Terminology

A [TIPS Ladder](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#tips-ladder) is a [Bond Ladder](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bond-ladder) built from TIPS,
its targets and amounts stated in real terms, so [DARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#dara) takes the place of
[DAA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#daa), and [ARA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ara) that of [AA](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#aa)
(1.0 §Bond Ladder Concepts).

Also used throughout: [Funded Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#funded-year) and its synonym [Rung](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#rung),
[Maturity Year](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#maturity-year),
[LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), and [Available Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#available-cash).

<a id="maturity-selection"></a>
## Maturity Selection Within a Funded Year

A funded year may hold **one or more TIPS** — DARA remains an *annual* target; the year's real P+I
`need` is *split across* whichever TIPS the funded year holds (see §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split
below). Only [outstanding](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#outstanding-tips) TIPS are
candidates — an auctioned-but-unissued TIPS is never selectable, in any mode, by any policy.

`maturityPref` controls which TIPS fill each year, purely from `tipsMarketData` (no holdings involved —
build and rebalance share the same selection via `selectLadderBonds`). It applies to every funded
year unless the TIPS for a particular year have been picked individually (§Picking Maturities for a
Funded Year):

- **`last`** *(default)* — one TIPS, the latest-maturing in the year. Legacy behavior; byte-for-byte
  unchanged output.
- **`first`** — one TIPS, the earliest-maturing in the year.
- **`semiannual`** — the Jan TIPS + the Jul TIPS (when both exist), Jan→Jul order. A year with only
  one of the two (e.g. single-Feb 2040+) falls back to the one TIPS available.
- **`all`** — one TIPS per distinct maturity **month**, earliest→latest. Never both same-month
  issues — see coupon tie-break below. (How many months a year offers follows the issuance
  calendar, not a fixed schedule: see DATA_DICTIONARY §5.1 §Issuance-Dependent Values.)

**Same-month, two-issue tie-break (`couponPref`):** some months have two TIPS issues with different
coupons (Jan 2027/28/29, Apr 2028/29). `couponPref` (`'higher'` default, or `'lower'`) selects which
one wins whenever a policy must pick a single TIPS for that month — including `all`, which never
holds both. CUSIP is the final tiebreak (stable, independent of `tipsMarketData` iteration order) when
coupons tie exactly. **`last` never needs this**: the duplicate-issue months (Jan, Apr) are never
the latest month in their year, so the tie-break is unreachable under `last`.

**Shared with Rebalance, except `couponPref`.** `maturityPref` (this control, and its fifth choice
Select maturities) is shared UI, visible and live in both modes — Rebalance's *target* side reads the
same `_canon.yearTipsListMap[year]` this section describes, via `selectLadderBonds`, rather than
defaulting to a hardcoded `'last'`. Which one of a year's candidates a rebalance actually buys into
or sells from each run is a separate, *ordering* question, decided by §Within-Year Allocation Policy
below — nothing in *this* section is specific to Build, so Rebalance's candidate set uses it exactly
as written. Opening the Select-maturities picker in Rebalance seeds its checkboxes from **current
holdings** for that year (not `buildDetails`, which has nothing to show pre-Build) — a held CUSIP the
user unchecks there is a deliberate sell, not a silent default. `couponPref` remains **Build-only**:
it is never restored on the rebalance side, at load or via the picker — Rebalance always resolves a
same-month two-issue collision with the `'higher'` default (4.0 §ladder-core.js `selectLadderBonds`).

### Picking Maturities for a Funded Year

When none of the `maturityPref` choices gives the wanted result, the TIPS for a funded year may be
picked individually instead, via a fifth, **mutually exclusive** Maturity preference choice: **Select
maturities**. It is not a policy layered under `last`/`first`/`semiannual`/`all` — choosing it
replaces whichever policy was active, and opens a picker modal (one column per calendar month,
Low/High/All/None bulk actions per column) where individual TIPS are checked per year.

**Picking maturities is a dictate, not a preference.** A funded year with no explicit pick is not
filled by any `maturityPref` fallback — its DARA is forced to $0, so it is structurally present
(still resolved for bracket/gap classification) but funds nothing that year. This is the same
precedence a manual $0 per-year DARA entry already has. So Select maturities is an all-or-nothing
switch for the years it covers: every year in range either has an explicit pick or is intentionally
empty — there is no partial "policy-fill the years I didn't touch" behavior.

**The ladder's actual first AND last year, while Select maturities is active and at least one pick
has been made, are the earliest and latest years with an explicit pick** — the First/Last Year
fields no longer drive anything, in **both Build and Rebalance**. Rather than hide the fields (an
earlier design that gave no way to see what range the picks had actually settled on), they stay
visible but **disabled**, their displayed value kept in sync with the picked range — a read-only
mirror, not an input, while Select maturities is active. `_effectiveYearRange` (index.html) is the
single source both the picker's own offered year range and the Build/Rebalance run read, so the
picker, the per-year DARA panel, and the actual run can never drift out of sync with each other the
way a field left un-synced to the pick set previously could (e.g. the per-year panel continuing to
show a rung stretching to a stale Last Year value the picks had already made irrelevant). With no
picks yet — just switched into Select, nothing checked — both fields fall back to whatever was set
before switching, giving the picker a starting canvas to work from.

**Widening or narrowing the range once picks exist means leaving Select maturities.** Since the
fields are disabled while picks exist, the only way to reach a year outside the current picked span
is to switch to a named policy (`last`/`first`/`semiannual`/`all`), change First/Last Year there,
then switch back to Select maturities. **Picks are preserved across that round trip** — switching
away no longer discards them (an earlier design cleared them), so returning to Select maturities
restores exactly what was picked before, now against the wider or narrower canvas the field edit
established. (Any per-year DARA the app had force-zeroed for an unpicked year IS cleared on
switching away, since that value belongs to the named policy now active, not to the remembered
pick set.)

Each selected maturity carries an **allocation weight** (see §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split). Equal
weights — the default, and the only weights the selection UI writes today — reproduce the even split
exactly.

`couponPref` does not apply to a year whose TIPS were picked individually — when a month carries two
issues, the one that was picked is the one that is bought. It still governs every year filled by
`maturityPref` when Select maturities is not the active choice.

Maturities may be picked for a bracket year too. The bracket role stays with the latest-maturing
TIPS picked for that year (§Gap Years), so picking maturities can move the lower bracket duration match.
Holding one maturity purely as bracket excess while a different maturity funds the year is
legitimate.

**Not yet persisted.** Per-year picks are prototype-only: they do not round-trip through the
Holdings export or the `#params` block (see 4.2 Import holdings and DARA plan) — reloading or importing a file
loses them, same as any other in-session-only UI state today.

### Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split

When a funded year holds N ≥ 1 TIPS (its `yearTipsListMap[year]` list, ordered by maturity), the
year's real P+I need is spread across them **by allocation weight**:

```
need    = yearDARA − runningLMI − excessLMI − future30yExtra    // same need as the N=1 case
share_i = need × w_i / Σw
for each TIPS i (earliest → latest), except the last:
  qty_i = MAX(0, ROUND(share_i / piPerBond_i))
last TIPS absorbs the rounding remainder:
  qty_last = MAX(0, ROUND((need − Σ delivered P+I of the other TIPS) / piPerBond_last))
```

**Equal weights are the default**, and reduce to `share = need / N` — the even split. Weights exist
so that uneven allocation within a year (more maturing in January than in October, say) is a change
of inputs rather than a second formula; there is one split rule, not one per case.

Each sub-rung is sized from its **own** P+I (`piPerBond_i` differs by coupon/maturity); the latest
maturity in the year absorbs the remainder so the year's total lands on `need`. Same-year sub-rungs
never feed each other's LMI — they mature in the same year, so a single year-level `need` is the
correct thing to split (not N independent per-TIPS DARAs). `N = 1` reproduces the legacy single-TIPS
sizing exactly, for any weight. The year's aggregate `fundedYearQty` and `annualInterest` (for LMI)
are the sum across sub-rungs.

**Spec reference:** 4.0 §`sizeYearRungs` / `aggregateRungs` (ladder-core.js).

<a id="within-year-allocation-policy"></a>
### Within-Year Allocation Policy

`maturityPref` (above) and per-year picks decide which TIPS are **candidates** for a funded year;
this section decides which candidate a **rebalance** actually trades each run, whenever more than
one is in play (multiple maturity months under `all`/`semiannual`, an explicit multi-TIPS pick, or
simply because current holdings differ from what `maturityPref` would pick fresh today). Build never
needs this — §Multi-TIPS Funded-Year Split splits a build's `need` evenly across every candidate at
once, since Build starts from nothing; Rebalance starts from an existing portfolio, so a buy or sell
has to land on one real holding, not be spread pro-rata across TIPS that may not even be held.

**Never manufactures a trade.** Everything below only ever decides *where* an already-necessary buy
or sell lands — never *whether* one happens. A year whose real P+I `need` (3.0 §Named Quantities &
Formulas) is unchanged is never touched, no matter how unevenly its held maturities compare under any
policy (3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution applies this at the formula level).

**Three ladder-wide policies**, each an ordering rule over that year's candidate ∪ held union
(highest buy/hold-priority first) — the concrete mechanism is `rankForYear()`, 4.0 §allocation-policy.js:

- **Maturity order** — ranks by maturity date. Direction follows the top-level Maturity preference:
  latest-maturing wins ties under `'last'`, earliest-maturing under `'first'` (`'all'`/`'semiannual'`/
  `'select'` have no direction of their own and fall back to `'last'`'s latest-wins convention).
- **SA-Yield order** — ranks by SA yield descending: highest preferred to hold/buy first, lowest
  sells first. A missing SA yield never outranks a real one; ties fall back to the maturity rule
  above.
- **Equal split** — no fixed preference at all. **Levels** the year's maturities toward equal value
  rather than draining one before the next (`levelValues()`, 4.0 §allocation-policy.js): growth
  water-fills onto the currently-lowest-value candidate(s), shrinkage drains the currently-highest-
  value one(s), re-leveling at every step so a single CUSIP is never overshot past parity. A
  not-yet-held candidate has value 0, so it always ranks first for a buy — it's being filled to catch
  up, not preferred over an existing holding.

**Locked to Maturity order under a fixed Maturity preference.** `'last'`/`'first'` already commit
every funded year to one fixed direction — letting Equal split or SA-Yield order apply *on top of*
that reintroduces exactly the ambiguity this feature exists to remove. The Allocation policy control
is disabled and forced to Maturity order whenever Maturity preference is `'last'`/`'first'`, and live
(defaulting to Equal split) whenever it is `'all'`/`'semiannual'`/`'select'`, which have no single
fixed direction of their own (6.0 §Row 2 — Construction policy).

**Per-year override.** For one specific year, an explicit priority order set via the priority-order
picker (a modal reachable from the Allocation policy dropdown's "Set priority order…" choice — 6.0
§Priority-Order Picker) replaces whichever global policy is active, for that year only. Picking a
named global policy clears every existing per-year override — the global choice and any override are
mutually exclusive at a given moment, never blended.

**Scope.** The *excess* CUSIP a bracket/gap/cover year carries is never chosen here — bracket/gap/cover
CUSIP identification (§Duration Matching, §Retained Bracket Excess) never calls `rankForYear` for it,
and its sell-oldest-first rule is untouched by any policy above. A bracket year that is also a funded
year does call `rankForYear` for its *own funded need* (3.0 §Target CUSIP resolution), restricted to
Maturity order only (never Equal split or SA-Yield order, which are ordinary-year cash-flow-preference
concepts the bracket/duration-match machinery does not consult) — and even then, "never manufactures a
trade" above still governs: if the excess CUSIP's own existing holding already funds the year's need in
full, nothing moves, exactly as for an ordinary year.

<a id="gap-years-section"></a>
## Gap Years

[Gap Years](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#gap-years) are covered synthetically in Build, and by bracket excess in a held ladder. Treasury issues the missing 10-year TIPS in later years, at which point those years leave the gap years and fund themselves.

<a id="synthetic-tips-for-gap-years"></a>
**Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years:**
Created to maintain ladder continuity and facilitate duration matching.

**A synthetic TIPS is modeled as if it were issued today** — auctioned and settled at the
current date, rather than at the future date Treasury will actually issue it. This is the assumption
the whole gap calculation rests on, and it is what makes a synthetic rung comparable to the real TIPS
on either side of it: its index ratio is taken as 1.00000, so every figure it contributes is
denominated in the same Ref CPI basis as every bond actually held. Nothing about a gap rung is
projected forward, and the ladder never assumes a future rate of inflation.

- **Maturity**: January 15 of the gap year. 10-year TIPS mature in January and July, and the January
  issue is the first to reach that year. (The Future 30Y synthetics use February 15 instead, matching
  the 30-year issue month — see §Future 30Y Rungs.)
- **Index ratio**: taken as 1.00000, an approximation (DD [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips)).
- **Coupon**: the 1/8 percent at or below the interpolated yield, floored at 0.125%. This mirrors how
  Treasury sets a new issue’s coupon at auction, and the floor is Treasury policy — no marketable
  Treasury security, TIPS included, carries a coupon below 0.125%.
- **Price**: computed from the synthetic’s own yield and coupon, not assumed to be par. Because the
  coupon is the eighth at or below the yield, a security issued today prices **at or just below par**
  whenever the real yield is positive, reaching exactly par only when the yield lands on an eighth. It
  prices **above** par when a negative real yield meets the 0.125% coupon floor, and materially so:
  at a real yield of −1% the coupon floor puts the price roughly 11 points over par. Assuming 100
  would bias Gap Total Cost in one direction in ordinary conditions and badly in that one.

**Gap Parameter Calculation:**
Synthetic TIPS are processed **longest to shortest** to model a hypothetical ladder as if gap TIPS existed. This establishes the **Gap Total Cost** (used for bracket sizing) and **Gap Avg Duration** (used for duration matching).

**Anchor when firstYear is inside the gap:** `anchorBefore` is always the **most recently issued** 10-year TIPS that is [outstanding](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#outstanding-tips), i.e. the longest-dated 10-year maturity available below `minGapYear`. When `firstYear` is a gap year after the first gap year, `minGapYear` is `firstYear` (no 10-year TIPS exists for the gap years before it), so the code walks backward from `minGapYear − 1` until it reaches that anchor.

**LMI for gap years** uses the same definition as for any other rung: annual coupon interest from ALL TIPS maturing after the gap year. This includes:
- **Actual TIPS LMI**: annual interest from funded years above the gap year. When `preLadderInterest` is active, only funded years with non-zero TIPS purchased contribute (zeroed funded years have no actual TIPS and therefore no coupon income).
- **Synthetic LMI** (`runningSynLMI`): accumulated synthetic interest from longer gap years already processed in this sweep (same accumulator pattern as the main ladder).

**Pre-Ladder Interest (PLI) — single interleaved pass** *(Build and Rebalance, when `preLadderInterest` enabled)*:
The PLI pool is distributed across ALL years in maturity order (short→long), with funded years and gap years interleaved. Gap years consume pool before longer-dated funded years (the gap years are processed before 2040), so whether a funded year like 2040 gets zeroed depends on how much pool the gap years consumed first.
```
remaining = preLadderPool
for year in allYears ascending (short→long):   // allYears = rangeYears ∪ gapYears, sorted
  if year is a funded year:
    need = MAX(0, DARA - prelim[year].laterMatInt)
    if remaining >= need:
      zeroedFundedYears.add(year)
      remaining -= need
    else:
      partialCreditYear = year; partialCredit = remaining; remaining = 0; break
  if year is a gap year:
    need = MAX(0, DARA - actualTIPSLMI(year))   // full prelim LMI for all funded years above; synth LMI not yet known
    if remaining >= need:
      pliCredit[year] = need
      remaining -= need
    else:
      pliCredit[year] = remaining
      remaining = 0; break
```
After this pass, `zeroedFundedYears` is fully determined (including whether bracket years like 2040 are zeroed).

**Steps** (long→short qty sweep):
1. **Anchors**: `anchorBefore` (most recently issued 10-year TIPS) and `anchorAfter` (earliest Feb TIPS after gap).
2. **Interpolate Yield**: Linear interpolation between anchors using maturity dates.
3. **Synthetic Coupon**: `MAX(0.00125, FLOOR(yield * 100 / 0.125) * 0.00125)` — the eighth at or below the interpolated yield, floored at Treasury’s 0.125% minimum coupon.
4. **Duration**: Calculate Modified Duration for each synthetic TIPS.
5. **PI per Synthetic TIPS**: `PI_y = 1,000 × (1 + coupon / 2)` (nPeriods = 1, Jan maturity; in dollars). Index ratio is taken as 1.00000, so this is both the real and the Ref-CPI-basis figure.
6. **Required Quantity**: `qty_y = MAX(0, ROUND((yearDARA_y − runningSynLMI − actualTIPSLMI − pliCredit[y]) / PI_y))` where `yearDARA_y = daraByYear.get(y) ?? DARA`.
   - `runningSynLMI` = accumulated synthetic interest from longer gap years already processed in this sweep.
   - `actualTIPSLMI` = sum of annual coupon interest from all actual funded years above this gap year.
   - `pliCredit[y]` = PLI pool credit allocated to this gap year (0 when `preLadderInterest` is disabled).
   - Synthetic TIPS in ladder 1 are constructed exactly like any other rung: LMI and PLI credit reduce the required quantity the same way they do for actual funded years.
7. **LMI Update**: `runningSynLMI += qty_y × 1,000 × coupon`. This accumulated synthetic interest **does** feed into the `runningSynLMI` term for shorter gap years in subsequent iterations.
8. **Cost**: `cost_y = qty_y × Cost per TIPS`, where Cost per TIPS = (`price_y` ÷ 100) × Index Ratio × 1,000 with the Index Ratio taken as 1.00000 (DD Synthetic TIPS, Cost per TIPS), and `price_y` is the clean price implied by the synthetic’s own yield and coupon at the current settlement date (`priceFromYield`, 4.0 §bond-math.js). A security issued today carries no accrued interest, so the clean price is the whole cost. Summed over the gap years this is **Gap Total Cost**, which sizes the bracket excess (§Duration Matching Brackets).

## Gap Year Coverage Model

The gap coverage algorithm can be understood as two equivalent constructions:

**Component 1 — Synthetic ladder:**
Every year in the ladder — funded years and gap years alike — is treated as a rung. Gap years receive synthetic TIPS (hypothetical, never purchased) sized so each gap year delivers ~DARA, exactly like any funded year. Each synthetic TIPS generates annual coupon that flows as LMI to shorter-maturity rungs, including the funded year immediately below the gap years. No excess exists anywhere in this view.

**Component 2 — Excess TIPS:**
Same construction, but gap years have no TIPS yet. Treasury issues each one in January, ten years before its maturity year, so the model converts each Synthetic TIPS into a [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips) on its issue date (§Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS). Until a Synthetic TIPS is converted, its cost is carried by [Excess TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#excess-tips) at the lower and upper bracket years, split by [Bracket Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-weight), and each conversion sells the Excess TIPS that the Synthetic TIPS not yet converted no longer need. The last conversion falls years before the first gap year, so the Excess TIPS pays no interest in a gap year. Its interest is credited to the funded years in which it is paid (§Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS, §Interest credited to a funded year).

**LMI is always annual coupon interest only — never principal.** P+I (principal + last-year interest received at maturity) is a separate concept applied only to the funded year that holds the TIPS. Excess bracket bonds never contribute their principal to another rung's LMI.

**Excess coupon always flows inward:** Excess bracket coupon always contributes to LMI for shorter-maturity rungs within the ladder. The shortest rung in the ladder has no shorter rungs to receive the flow — the coupon is applied where shorter rungs exist, and there is no "exit" from the ladder.

**Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only.** Every rung's incoming LMI is the full annual coupon (both semiannual payments) from every holding above it, *except* the one funded year that contains `settlementDate`: that year is already partway through its calendar year, so a coupon date already paid is not still-forthcoming cash for that year, regardless of what happened to the cash afterward. For that one rung only, each contributing holding's coupon is `remainingCouponPaymentsThisYear(maturity, settlementDate, bondHolidays, tradeDate) × couponPerPeriod × principalPerBond`, where `remainingCouponPaymentsThisYear` counts how many of that holding's two annual coupon dates, within `settlementDate`'s own calendar year, are still unpaid as of `tradeDate` (0, 1, or 2) — the same coupon-date walk the Cash Flow Calendar uses (`shared/src/bond-math.js` `couponSchedule`/`_nextCouponOnOrAfter`), so the two never disagree. Every other funded year, gap year, and bracket year is entirely in the future and keeps the full-annual figure; this affects funded-year sizing (`sizeLadder`'s corrected sweep) and both Before/After ARA recovery (`runRebalance`'s `araLaterMaturityInterestByYear` and `rebuildLaterMatInt`) identically, so Build and Rebalance stay consistent for that year.

**Consequence: the current rung grows as the year goes on.** Because coupons already paid stop counting toward it, the settlement year's incoming LMI shrinks with every coupon date that passes, and the rung needs correspondingly more principal to reach the same DARA. A rebalance run in February can therefore ask to buy into the current year when the identical portfolio, priced the previous December, asked for nothing — the difference is when in the year the rebalance was run, not inflation, not the yield curve, and not any change in holdings. This is correct and not a rounding artifact; it is worth recognizing when reading a trade list, because it appears alongside causes that look similar and are not.

**"Unpaid" is checked against `tradeDate` (today), not `settlementDate`.** `settlementDate` is T+1 from today — the settlement of a *hypothetical new trade* placed today, which is what every other calculation in this app correctly prices off. But "has this coupon already been paid" is a fact about today, not about a trade that hasn't happened, and using `settlementDate` for it excludes a coupon on the very day it actually pays: a coupon due Saturday Aug 15 rolls to Monday Aug 17, and if today is Aug 17 the cash is already in hand, but `settlementDate` (Aug 18) would still call it "unpaid" for one more day, understating the settlement year's available income for that day. `tradeDate` defaults to `settlementDate` when a caller doesn't pass it (tests, and every call site predating this distinction); production Build/Rebalance runs always pass the real trade date (index.html `_tradeDateStr`, matching 5.0 §Cash Flow Calendar's own `today` — the two are the same date by construction, so the ladder and the Cash Flow Calendar always agree on which coupons have paid, letting the ladder's Amount popup be compared directly against a brokerage statement on the actual payment date).

A coupon date landing on a weekend or bond-market holiday isn't actually paid until the next bond trading day (5.0 §Cash Flow Calendar), so "unpaid as of `tradeDate`" is checked against that rolled-forward payment date, not the raw semiannual date — `remainingCouponPaymentsThisYear` takes `bondHolidays` (`shared/src/settlement.js` `parseHolidaySet`) and applies the same `actualPaymentDate()` roll used by the Cash Flow Calendar, so a coupon scheduled for a Saturday isn't dropped from LMI three days before the cash is actually in hand. `bondHolidays` defaults to an empty Set (weekend rolling alone, no observed-holiday rolling) when a caller doesn't have holiday data loaded; production Build/Rebalance runs always pass the real set.

### Maturity Proceeds Are Spent

A ladder exists so that each rung's principal is **applied toward** its funded year's Amount. The app assumes exactly that: once a holding matures it stops appearing in the market data, drops out of the portfolio silently, and its principal is not carried forward as available funds. Net Cash for a rebalance run shortly after a maturity therefore reports a purchase against money the holder no longer has — which is right, because by assumption it has already been applied.

A holder who has **not** applied it says so with [Available Cash](#available-cash) below. That is the whole mechanism; there is no separate concept for maturity proceeds, accumulated coupons, or a distribution held back, because all of them are the same fact: cash on hand that will be applied toward a funded year's Amount rather than invested in TIPS.

### Available Cash

Cash sitting in the account is income the ladder does not have to buy bonds to produce. A rung exists
to deliver its funded year's Amount; if cash on hand will cover part or all of that Amount,
the rung needs correspondingly fewer TIPS, or none at all. **Available Cash** (`availableCash`,
dollars, default 0) states how much cash is on hand for that purpose.

**What the figure means.** Cash the holder will **apply toward** the funded years' Amounts rather than
invest in TIPS. Entering it says "I will draw on this instead of holding bonds for the early years,"
and the ladder is sized down accordingly.
Cash intended for the ladder instead is simply not entered: the rebalance sizes from DARA as usual,
Net Cash comes back negative by whatever the purchases cost, and the holder covers it. Those are the
two distinct things a holder can do with cash, and which one is meant is not inferable from the
balance, which is why the figure is entered rather than detected. It is not validated against holdings
or coupon history; the figure is taken as given. A non-zero value is itself the opt-in.

Sources are immaterial to the calculation and include, among others, a maturity not applied in its own
year, coupons that accumulated rather than being reinvested, cash set aside for a
required minimum distribution, and money not yet deployed into the ladder.

**Cash already received this year is offered as a starting figure.** A ladder loaded in Rebalance has
been held over part of the current year, and the cash flows it generated over that part have been
received. §Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment assumes settlement-year income is applied toward that
year's Amount rather than reinvested, so that money is on hand and belongs in this box. Two
sources count:

- **Coupons already paid**, on every holding.
- **Maturity proceeds.** A rung that has matured paid out its inflation-adjusted principal along with
  its final coupon, which *is* that year's Amount arriving. Rebalancing in August, a ladder holding
  January, April and July rungs has already collected three of them; the later in the year, the more
  of the rung is already in hand. A matured TIPS is no longer quoted and so is absent from the
  market data, which is why the rung would otherwise read as unfunded and be bought back — the app
  resolves it from the TIPS reference series (maturity, coupon, dated date Ref CPI: everything needed to value
  what it paid out, and nothing about a market value it no longer has).

Both are valued at each payment's own payment-date Ref CPI, through the same code path the Cash Flow
Calendar uses (5.0 §Cash Flow Calendar), so the two can never value one payment differently. The app counts it and fills
the box in, marked amber to show the figure is the app's rather than the holder's; typing over it
clears the mark and the entered value stands. A file that states its own `availableCash` has already
said what it holds and is left alone.

The figure includes the settlement-year coupons and maturity proceeds received after the **Ref CPI
date the file records** (3.0 §DARA Reference Date), the settlement Ref CPI date in effect when the file
was saved. Anything from an earlier year is never included, however far back the recorded date falls:
a rung funds its own year, and money received in an earlier year was applied toward that year. Which
of the two components are in play varies: the coupon half answers to the coupon-counting choice
below, and a file of current positions carries no matured rung to draw proceeds from, so the app
names the components it actually has rather than claiming both.

- A ladder saved today has generated nothing after its own date, so the figure is zero and the box
  stays empty. This is what keeps a same-day build → export → import round trip at zero trades; it
  falls out of the rule rather than being a special case.
- A ladder saved a year ago has generated every settlement-year payment to date, and that money is
  what funds its rebalance (3.0 §Funding the rebalance).
- A broker file states no date at all. Those holdings are a real position held all along, so every
  settlement-year payment is included. Because that is an assumption the holder cannot otherwise see,
  and it moves trades, the status strip says which payments were included and offers the
  alternatives. That choice is its own entry, separate from the file's Ref CPI date
  (3.0 §DARA Reference Date): it sets which payments this figure includes and restates nothing, and it is
  offered wherever the app filled this figure in from a dateless file, including a position file that
  carries no per-year amounts for a Ref CPI date to act on. A file recording its own `refCpiDate`
  still supplies both from that one date, unchanged.

**The starting point is a coupon payment date to count after, chosen from a list.** Every TIPS ever
issued matures on the 15th of January, February, April, July or October, so payments land on the 15th
of those months and August, and money reaches the holder on no other day. A starting point on any
other day names a day nothing happened on and includes exactly what the nearest payment date includes,
so the choices offered are the payment dates themselves: every settlement-year payment, or every
payment after each date this portfolio was actually paid on and that has already passed. The list is
built from the holdings' own payment schedules rather than from the fixed months, so it names only
dates this portfolio was paid on, and each choice is labelled with the amount it produces.

**Each choice is exclusive of the date it names.** "After the July 15 coupon payment date" excludes
the July 15 payment and includes every later one. Stating the starting point as a payment rather than a date
settles what an inclusive-or-not date could not say for itself. The comparison is against **scheduled**
payment dates, not the shifted ones: a payment due on a weekend or holiday is made on the next business
day, and both are the same payment, so "after July 15" drops a July 15 coupon whether it was paid on
the 15th or the 17th.

**The choice and the figure are one control.** Clicking the Available Cash box opens a chooser
listing every starting point with the amount it produces, and, as its last entry, a plain amount to
state outright. The box itself displays the result and takes no typing: the payment dates are what
make the counted figure, so offering them anywhere other than where the figure is set leaves two
controls competing for one row of the card, which is what a link beside the box turned out to be.
The status strip also offers the chooser on load, where the assumption first needs stating, and its
notice is refreshed when the choice changes so it cannot describe a figure that is no longer shown.

Picking a starting point asks for the app's figure back, overriding an amount the holder had stated:
picking one is asking for the figure it produces. Stating an amount remains the way to express what
the payment dates cannot, a matured rung absent from a broker file among them.

**The help leads with the figures.** The Available Cash help opens with one row per settlement-year
payment date already made, its coupons and its principal, and the total that becomes the figure in the
box; rows before the starting point, and the coupon column when the coupon-counting choice excludes it, are
struck through rather than omitted, so what was left out is visible next to what was kept. The prose
explaining all of it follows. The rungs that matured are named at the end, where a CUSIP per row would
otherwise crowd the figures.

**A file's own Ref CPI date maps onto one of these choices.** A ladder saved part-way through the
settlement year has generated every payment from that date forward, which is what "after the last
payment date before it" describes; a date in an earlier year excludes nothing, so every settlement-year
payment is included. That mapped choice is also the earliest the list offers, since a holder cannot
have been collecting from before the ladder was saved.

**Maturity proceeds are only recoverable from a file this app saved.** A broker position file lists
what is currently held, and a matured bond is not currently held, so it is simply absent and there is
nothing to value. Our own Holdings export still carries the matured CUSIP and its quantity, which is
why the proceeds can be computed there. From a broker import the offer is coupons only, and matured
principal is the holder's to enter.

**The coupon half follows the coupon-counting choice, live.** Whether the settlement year's coupons
were kept toward that year or reinvested is a single question, already asked by §Settlement-Year
Coupon Treatment's control. This box answers to it rather than asking again, and moves the moment it
changes:

- `'all'` — coupons are applied toward this year, so every one already paid is counted.
- `'last'` — every coupon but the final one is reinvested, and each already-paid coupon is by
  definition earlier than the last one still to arrive, so none are counted.
- `'none'` — all are reinvested, so none are counted.

**Maturity proceeds do not follow it.** Returned principal is not coupon income, and a rung exists to
mature and be applied toward its year's Amount, so it counts under every setting. The two halves are governed separately for
that reason.

**The offer stops entirely once the holder states a figure**, including clearing the box to nothing.
From then on the app does not touch it, and a later change to the coupon-counting control leaves it
alone: an entered figure is a statement, and the app must not overwrite one.

This does not contradict "entered rather than detected" above: the starting point comes from a date the file
states, not from an inference about a cash balance, and the offered figure is a default the holder
overwrites at will.

**Scale.** On a ladder built a year ago holding every maturity month, three 2026 rungs have matured by
late August. Ignoring their proceeds forces the self-financing scale (3.0 §Funding the rebalance) to
cut ARA by 6.1% to pay for principal the holder had already been paid; counting them, the same
rebalance costs 1.3%. Holding only the earliest maturity in each year makes it starker still: one
matured rung, 7.1% against 0.8%.

**Rebalance only.** The figure states what the holder will apply toward the Amounts out of a portfolio
they already hold. A Build starts from cash by definition and reports what the ladder costs, so there is nothing
for it to mean there: the control is not shown in Build, and a Build run always sizes at zero cash —
including when a DARA plan carrying a figure from Rebalance is imported into Build, which must not
apply unseen. The state is kept per-mode alongside every other figure on the card.

**How it is consumed.** Available Cash is a pool applied to the **earliest funded year first**, moving
up the ladder until it is exhausted, exactly as the [Pre-Ladder Interest](#pre-ladder-interest) pool
is. Where the pool covers a year's whole need, that rung is funded entirely from cash and buys no
TIPS; where it runs out partway through a year, that year takes the remainder as a partial credit and
every later year is untouched. A rung funded entirely from cash pays no coupon, so it contributes
nothing to any shorter year's [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi) — the same treatment a
pre-ladder-zeroed rung gets.

**One pool, two sources.** Available Cash and Pre-Ladder Interest are separate inputs that feed a
single short-to-long pass, so there is one consumption mechanism rather than two competing ones. They
are kept as separate inputs because their provenance differs — pre-ladder interest is derived from
coupon history and gated on a ladder that starts in a future year, while available cash is a figure
the holder asserts and applies whenever it is non-zero. Available cash is consumed **first**, being
money already in hand against coupons still to arrive, and each year's credit records the two
components separately so a drill can show which dollars came from where.

**The settlement year is measured against its remaining coupons.** The pass reaches the settlement
year first, and that year's need is `DARA − remaining-coupon LMI`, not `DARA −` a full year's LMI
(§Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only). Coupons already paid this year are already in hand
and are not still-forthcoming income for that rung, so charging the pool against a full-year figure
would understate what the settlement year actually needs and spill cash up the ladder too early.

**Relationship to the superseded RMD cash override.** This replaces `rmdCashOverride`, which credited
the same kind of figure but only to the settlement year and discarded any surplus beyond that year's
need. For any amount at or below the settlement year's need the two are identical, so existing files
and habits carry over unchanged; above it, cash that used to vanish now funds the next rung up. A
required minimum distribution needs no mechanism of its own: cash held back for one is cash that will
be applied toward this year's Amount, which is what this figure already means.

**Persistence**: rides the `#params` line of both the Holdings export and the standalone DARA-plan
file (2.1 §Standalone DARA-plan file) as `availableCash=<dollars>`, written only when non-zero. The
superseded `rmdCashOverride=<dollars>` key is still **read** as an alias so files written before this
change keep working; it is no longer written.

**UI**: an input in the ladder parameters alongside **Pre-ladder int.** (6.0 §Row 1), since the figure
applies to the whole ladder rather than to any one year.

### Settlement-Year Coupon Treatment

Whether a coupon not yet paid this year is still available cash by the time it is needed depends on
what happens to it in the meantime — reinvested into the ladder, or held in a money market fund or
short-term Treasury bill — and that is not something the app can infer from the calendar. This is a
forward assumption, and it applies only to the settlement year, the only funded year where the
already-paid/not-yet-paid split is ever ambiguous.

**Count remaining coupons** (`rmdCouponMode`, default `'all'`):
  - `'all'` — every remaining coupon this year counts (the settlement-year LMI figure above, unmodified). Matches holding all of this year's coupon cash in cash/short Treasuries through the target date.
  - `'last'` — only coupons landing on the single latest remaining coupon date this year count, **across every bond contributing to the settlement year's LMI pool, not each bond's own latest date**. A ladder routinely holds bonds on different semiannual cycles (Feb/Aug, Apr/Oct, ...), so by mid-year almost every contributing bond already has only one coupon left — "each bond's own last" would then be indistinguishable from `'all'` for nearly every bond. `'last'` instead asks a pool-wide question: of every remaining coupon date across every contributing bond, keep only the ones on the latest such date; a bond whose own only remaining coupon is earlier than another bond's is treated as already reinvested, the same as if it were `'none'` for that bond. Matches reinvesting every remaining coupon except the very last one to arrive before the target date.
  - `'none'` — no remaining coupon counts; the settlement year sizes off principal and Available Cash alone. Matches reinvesting every remaining coupon.

The pool-wide date for `'last'` (`ladder-core.js` `latestRemainingCouponDate`) is computed once per sizing pass, over every bond that pass's settlement-year LMI pool draws from — `sizeLadder` over the funded/bracket-year bonds in `rangeYears`, `runRebalance`'s Before-state over currently HELD bonds, its After-state over the canonical TARGET bonds (`selectLadderBonds`) — then passed into every `rmdCappedRemainingCoupons` call in that pass so every bond is judged against the same date. The setting feeds the settlement year's income the same way the coupon-based LMI does: `incomingLMI`/`excessLMI` are computed from `rmdCouponMode`-filtered remaining-coupon counts instead of the raw count, and the resulting need is what the Available Cash pool is charged against (§Available Cash). Applied identically in `sizeLadder`'s corrected sweep, `runRebalance`'s Before-state ARA recovery, and its After-state rebuild sweep, and echoed into `fundedYearAmount`'s displayed Amount so Before/After totals stay consistent with the sized quantity. Defaults (`0`, `'all'`) reproduce the settlement-year LMI figure above exactly — a caller that never sets them sees no behavior change.

A holder who takes a distribution early in the year rather than at year-end expresses that with the same two inputs, not a separate mechanism: Available Cash is whatever is already on hand from earlier-year coupons, and `rmdCouponMode: 'none'` sends every not-yet-paid coupon to next year's rung instead of this year's.

**Rebalance only.** The choice divides the settlement year into coupons already received and coupons still to arrive, and a Build has no first half: the ladder is bought on the build date, so every coupon from that date forward is its own income. That is `'all'`, and a Build sizes at it unconditionally, whatever a loaded file or the Rebalance side of the same session holds. The control is not rendered in Build, on the same grounds as Available Cash (§Available Cash §Rebalance only).

**Each half states what the other does.** This control and the received-cash window (§Available Cash) are the two halves of one settlement year, and each was previously silent about the other's existence. The popover says which coupons have already been received, that they are counted in Available Cash rather than here, and that this choice governs whether they count at all. It also says how many payment dates are left in the year, since that is what decides whether the choices still differ: once one date remains, `'all'` and `'last'` name the same coupon, and once none remains all three count nothing. The figures shown are the live ones for the loaded portfolio, not examples.

**UI**: a link on the settlement year's group header row in Rebalance (5.0 §Funded Year Group Header Row) opens a small popover holding this one setting and the note above. Every other year's row is unaffected, since no other funded year has a partly-elapsed calendar year to resolve. **Persistence**: rides the `#params` line (2.1 §Standalone DARA-plan file) as `rmdCouponMode=<all|last|none>`, written only when non-default — a plan saved without touching it round-trips as a plain `#fundedYear,dara` file. A file carrying a non-default value still restores it in Rebalance; a Build ignores it, as it ignores a carried Available Cash figure.

<a id="gap-year-simulated-outstanding-tips"></a>
## Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS

The gap years have no outstanding TIPS yet. In [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) each is a [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips). In reality Treasury auctions a 10-year TIPS every January and July and issues it on the last trading day of that month, and a Synthetic TIPS of a gap year matures in January (§Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years), so each gap year gets an outstanding TIPS in turn. The model follows that, as it does for the Future 30Y years (§Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS). On the issue date, the Synthetic TIPS of a gap year becomes a [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips), and the [Excess TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#excess-tips) is sold down as the conversions proceed. [Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2) then credits each funded year with the interest the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS and the Excess TIPS pay in that year.

### Issue dates

A gap year's TIPS is issued on the last trading day of January, 10 years before its maturity year. A TIPS maturing in January 2039 is issued on the last trading day of January 2029. The first gap year of the ladder is converted on the first issue date after settlement. The conversions are taken in order, earliest issue date first.

### What happens on each issue date

Each conversion is a rebalance. Everything is measured on the issue date. The Synthetic TIPS keep the yield and coupon Pass 1 gave them, and each bracket TIPS keeps its yield and coupon.

1. The Synthetic TIPS not yet issued are the gap years later than the one being issued. Their average duration is cost-weighted (§Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted).
2. Both bracket TIPS are older than at the last conversion, so their modified durations are shorter. The [Bracket Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bracket-weight) of each is solved again from the two durations, as in §2-Bracket Weights, and held within 0 to 1.
3. The Excess TIPS each bracket should hold is the total cost of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued, times its Bracket Weight, divided by its cost per TIPS, rounded to a whole number of TIPS.
4. The Excess TIPS above that quantity is sold. No Excess TIPS is bought: a bracket that holds no more than that quantity keeps what it holds. The Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS is bought at the quantity Pass 1 sized. The [Net Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#net-cash) is small next to the cost of the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS, and may be positive or negative.

After the last conversion no Synthetic TIPS are left, so the Excess TIPS is 0. In a Rebalance the Retained Bracket Excess rule (§Retained Bracket Excess) decides which lower bracket Excess TIPS is sold on an issue date; none is bought, the active lower bracket included.

### Interest credited to a funded year

Each funded year before the first gap year is credited, in its [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), with three amounts, alongside the interest of every TIPS held that matures later. When the ladder also has Future 30Y years, these are in addition to the three amounts of §Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS, §Interest credited to a funded year.

- **From the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS.** A Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS is issued after its January 15 coupon date, so it pays one coupon in its issue year (July 15) and two in each later year. Each coupon is half the annual coupon, which is the quantity times 1,000 times the synthetic coupon. A funded year is credited with every Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS issued by that year.
- **From the Excess TIPS.** Each bracket TIPS pays on two coupon dates a year. The quantity on a coupon date is the one held on that date: a coupon date earlier in the year than the issue date is paid on the quantity held before that year's conversion, and a coupon date later in the year on the quantity held after it. A bracket TIPS that matures in January or July pays its January 15 coupon on the quantity before the conversion and its July 15 coupon on the quantity after it; one that matures in February or August pays both on the quantity after it. A year with no conversion pays both coupons on the same quantity. Before the first conversion the quantity is the one Pass 1 sized.
- **From the Net Cash.** The Net Cash of an issue date is cash delivered in the year of that date, so it is credited to the funded year of that year.

A coupon counts toward the funded year in which it is paid, whatever the month, whether it is an actual coupon or a simulated one. The number of coupons differs from year to year, so these amounts are worked out for each funded year directly and are not carried down in the running LMI that the sweep passes to the shorter years. In the settlement year only a coupon not yet paid counts (§Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only).

From the year after the last conversion the Excess TIPS is 0 and every Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS pays two coupons a year. That total is the figure Pass 1 uses as the LMI for the Synthetic TIPS.

**Pre-Ladder Interest.** The pool for the years before the first funded year (§Pre-Ladder Interest) includes all three amounts for those years.

<a id="future-30y-rungs-section"></a>
## Future 30Y Rungs

**Future 30Y Rungs:** Years in the ladder beyond the longest-dated issued TIPS maturity. They arise when the last year runs past that maturity, and reach at most the Maximum Future 30Y Rungs beyond it (DD §5.1; §Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS §Limit on the ladder's last year).

**Hypothetical TIPS for Future 30Y Rungs:**
Processed **longest to shortest** using the longest-dated issued TIPS as a flat-curve anchor:
- **Anchor yield**: Yield of the longest-dated issued TIPS, the most recently issued 30-year TIPS (flat-curve assumption).
- **Synthetic coupon**: `MAX(0.00125, FLOOR(yield × 100 / 0.125) × 0.00125)` — same formula as gap-year synthetic TIPS. Models the coupon a newly-issued 30Y TIPS would carry (nearest 0.125% below current yield); the actual coupon of the longest-dated issued TIPS is not used.
- **Maturity**: February 15 of the Future 30Y year (30-year TIPS are issued in February).
- **Index ratio**: taken as 1.00000, the same approximation as §Synthetic TIPS for Gap Years.
- **Price**: computed from the anchor yield and the synthetic coupon, not assumed to be par. Same rule and same reasoning as the gap synthetics: the coupon is the eighth at or below the yield, so the rung prices at or just below par at a positive real yield and above par when a negative real yield meets the 0.125% coupon floor.
- **Duration**: Calculate Modified Duration using the synthetic coupon and anchor yield.
- **Quantity and Cost**: Calculated identically to Gap Years (see above) using the flat-curve anchor.

**Recovering the last funded year on import.** The Future 30Y rungs aren't held as TIPS — they are represented by the **cover-pair excess** (excess at the lower cover and the upper cover), recorded in the import's excess column. So the intended last funded year is recovered by **reverse-engineering the Future 30Y duration match**: the cost-weighted split between upper- and lower-cover excess is a pure duration relationship — independent of DARA and increasing with the number of rungs — so the last funded year is the one whose cover split, computed exactly as the build computes it, reproduces the imported split. This is reliable because the excess column is a calculated field populated only by this app's export; the recovered year is user-overridable for the off-nominal case of a hand-edited file. When there is no cover excess, the ladder simply ends at the last issued TIPS. (The gap years recovers its *first* funded year from the same idea; that side can use a simpler excess-to-DARA ratio, but the ratio shortcut does **not** transfer to Future 30Y — its upper cover is deep-discount and a long run of Future 30Y years carries a large later-maturity-interest cascade — so the duration-match reversal is used here instead.)

<a id="future-30y-simulated-outstanding-tips"></a>
## Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS

The Future 30Y years have no outstanding TIPS yet. In [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) each is a [Synthetic TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#synthetic-tips). In reality Treasury auctions a 30-year TIPS every February and issues it on the last trading day of that month, so each Future 30Y year gets an outstanding TIPS in turn. The model follows that. On the issue date, the Synthetic TIPS of a Future 30Y year becomes a [Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-30y-tips), and the [Cover Excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cover-excess) is sold down as the conversions proceed. [Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2) then credits each funded year with the interest the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS and the Cover Excess pay in that year.

### Issue dates

A Future 30Y year's TIPS is issued on the last trading day of February, 30 years before its maturity year. A TIPS maturing in February 2066 is issued on the last trading day of February 2036. The first Future 30Y year is the year after the longest issued maturity year, and its issue date is the first February after settlement. The conversions are taken in order, earliest issue date first.

### What happens on each issue date

Each conversion is a rebalance. Everything is measured on the issue date. The Synthetic TIPS keep the yield and coupon Pass 1 gave them, and each cover keeps its yield and coupon.

1. The Synthetic TIPS not yet issued are the Future 30Y years later than the one being issued. Their average duration is cost-weighted (§Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted).
2. Both covers are older than at the last conversion, so their modified durations are shorter. The [Cover Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#cover-weight) of each cover is solved again from the two durations, as in §2-Bracket Weights, and held within 0 to 1.
3. Each cover's Cover Excess is set to the total cost of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued, times its Cover Weight, divided by its cost per TIPS, rounded to a whole number of TIPS.
4. The Cover Excess that falls is sold, the Cover Excess that rises is bought, and the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS is bought at the quantity Pass 1 sized. The [Net Cash](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#net-cash) is positive, apart from the rounding of quantities to whole TIPS: the Cover Excess is bought at a discount and rises in price toward par between issue dates, while the Synthetic TIPS are priced near par. It is the return of the Cover Excess, which takes the place of the coupons the Future 30Y TIPS would pay if all had been issued today ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).

After the last conversion no Synthetic TIPS are left, so the Cover Excess is 0.

### Interest credited to a funded year

Each funded year before the first Future 30Y year is credited, in its [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi), with three amounts, alongside the interest of every TIPS held that matures later.

- **From the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS.** A Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS is issued after its February 15 coupon date, so it pays one coupon in its issue year (August 15) and two in each later year. Each coupon is half the annual coupon, which is the quantity times 1,000 times the synthetic coupon. A funded year is credited with every Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS issued by that year. The first funded year therefore receives one coupon from the first, the second funded year receives two from the first and one from the next, and so on.
- **From the Cover Excess.** Each cover pays on February 15 and August 15. The February coupon is on the quantity held before that year's conversion, and the August coupon is on the quantity held after it. A year with no conversion pays both coupons on the same quantity. Before the first conversion the quantity is the one Pass 1 sized.
- **From the Net Cash.** The Net Cash of an issue date is cash delivered in the year of that date, so it is credited to the funded year of that year.

A coupon counts toward the funded year in which it is paid, whatever the month, whether it is an actual coupon or a simulated one. The number of coupons differs from year to year, so these amounts are worked out for each funded year directly and are not carried down in the running LMI that the sweep passes to the shorter years. In the settlement year only a coupon not yet paid counts (§Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only).

From the year after the last conversion the Cover Excess is 0 and every Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS pays two coupons a year. That total is the figure Pass 1 uses as the LMI for the Synthetic TIPS. While the yields of the covers are close to the anchor yield, the three amounts total about the full annual coupons of every Future 30Y TIPS over the years of the rebalances, as if all had been issued today, short by the accrued interest of the first coupon at settlement ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).

**Pre-Ladder Interest.** The pool for the years before the first funded year (§Pre-Ladder Interest) includes all three amounts for those years.

### Limit on the ladder's last year

The ladder's last year is at most the longest issued maturity year plus the Maximum Future 30Y Rungs (DD §5.1). No outstanding TIPS matures after the Future 30Y years, so each cover gets shorter with every conversion, and the TIPS that supplies the most duration is one with a very low coupon. The Maximum Future 30Y Rungs is the largest number of Future 30Y years for which, on every issue date, the average duration of the Synthetic TIPS not yet issued lies between the durations of the two covers. Beyond the Maximum Future 30Y Rungs, at some issue date the Cover Weight is held at 0 or 1 and the duration match is not reached, so a ladder with a later last year is not offered.

## Duration Matching (Brackets)

Gap years and Future 30Y years are covered by additional holdings in a **bracket pair** (or **cover pair**). The goal is for the excess holdings to change in value identically to the gap years if rates move.

**Modified duration throughout.** Every duration in this section — `d_lower`, `d_upper`, each rung's own duration, and the resulting `avgGapDuration` / future-30Y `avgDuration` — is modified duration (`calculateMDuration`, TIPS_Basics.md §Duration), not Macaulay. Modified duration is the price-sensitivity measure the weight solve needs: it is what makes the bracket pair's value move with the gap years's value under a rate shift.

### Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted
The representative duration of the gap years (`avgGapDuration` / future-30Y `avgDuration`) is the **cost-weighted** mean of its constituent synthetic rung durations — applied to **both** the gap years and the Future 30Y years:
```
avgGapDuration = Σ(rungCost × rungDur) / Σ rungCost,    rungCost = rung.qty × costPerBond
```
Cost-weighting is required because per-year DARA may be uneven: a more heavily-funded rung carries more cost and must pull the average toward its own duration. (A simple mean would mis-weight uneven funding.) When `Σ rungCost = 0`, fall back to the simple mean of rung durations.

### 2-Bracket Weights (Standard)
```
lowerWeight * d_lower + upperWeight * d_upper = avgGapDuration
upperWeight = CLAMP((avgGapDuration - d_lower) / (d_upper - d_lower), 0, 1)
lowerWeight = 1 - upperWeight
```
Weights are shares of the total cost being covered, so neither can be negative or exceed 1. If `avgGapDuration` falls outside `[d_lower, d_upper]`, clamp to the nearer cover instead of solving past it — a real corner case for the Future 30Y years: a deep-discount, near-zero-coupon cover (2052) can carry a longer duration than its own maturity would suggest, durationing out past a higher-coupon average. The coverage then lands entirely on the other cover, accepting a larger duration-match delta rather than a negative weight/quantity. See `bracketWeights` (4.0 §Computation Modules).

**Lower bracket always exists (when gap years are in range):** When `firstYear < 2040` and gap years are present, the lower bracket — the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket): the **latest-maturing** outstanding TIPS with maturity strictly below `minGapYear`, found by scanning `tipsMarketData` — always exists. **Latest-maturing, not "the latest January":** a pre-gap year may hold both a Jan and a Jul maturity, and the later one gives the better duration match, so it is the one used. Earlier maturities in that same year, if held, are funded-year holdings — they carry no bracket role. When `firstYear` is inside the gap, the lower bracket year is below `firstYear`; it is inserted into `yearBondMap`/`rangeYears` before the sweep with `fundedYearQty = 0` (pure bracket excess, no funded year component). Duration-matching always uses the standard 2-bracket formula with this lower bracket. When `firstYear ≥ 2040`, no gap years exist in the ladder range and bracket logic does not apply. **Rebalance**: the active lower bracket is always this canonical maturity, regardless of existing holdings. With **Retain lower bracket excess** off, any older lower bracket maturity (e.g. Jan 2034) falls into the normal rebalance sweep and is rebuilt to funded-year qty only; with it on, that excess is retained per §Retained Bracket Excess.

### Retained Bracket Excess (Rebalance)

Applies when holdings contain lower bracket excess in maturities **older** than the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket) — [Retained Bracket Excess](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#retained-bracket-excess). This arises naturally over time: each rebalance buys the then-active lower bracket, and when a later maturity is issued the previous one becomes retained, so the count of retained generations grows by one with each pre-gap issuance.

**The structure is not named by a count.** Any number of retained maturities may accumulate, so the arrangement is never named by how many brackets it holds — the rule below is the same for zero retained generations or five. `bracketWeightsN`, the solver below, already generalizes to any number of retained lower brackets; only the code that identifies *which* holdings are retained currently stops at one generation (see note at the end of this section).

Retained excess is not priced as though it sat at the active lower bracket's duration — it is older and therefore shorter, so doing that leaves the gap years under-matched (the realized blend lands short of `dGap`). Instead each retained maturity is **frozen at its own duration** and only the active lower bracket and the upper bracket are solved for:

```
R   = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r) / totalBlockCost           (retained share of that cost, frozen)
D_R = Σ (retainedExcessCost_r / totalBlockCost) × d_r     (retained lower brackets' duration contribution)

upperWeight       = ((dGap − D_R) − (1 − R) × d_activeLower) / (d_upper − d_activeLower)
activeWeight = (1 − R) − upperWeight
```

This is `bracketWeightsN` (4.0 §Computation Modules, `gap-math.js`) — the retained lower brackets enter the duration-match equation `Σ wᵣ·dᵣ + w_active·d_active + w_upper·d_upper = dGap` as fixed inputs; only `w_active` and `w_upper` are unknowns.

**Over-allocated** (`activeWeight` solves below the active lower bracket's own **floor** — see below — the lower brackets are carrying more than the gap years need):
- Sell the **earliest** retained maturity, and **only as much of it as restoring the match requires** — solve for the exact weight of that bracket that brings `activeWeight` back to exactly the floor, holding every other retained lower bracket fixed, and clamp to `[0, currently held]`. Selling a bracket out entirely when a partial sale would do is needless trading, not the rule.
- Only if depleting that bracket **entirely** is still not enough does the next-earliest retained maturity get the same treatment.
- Retained excess is **never bought** — buys land only in the active lower bracket and/or the upper bracket, which absorb whatever the retained lower brackets no longer cover.

**The active lower bracket's floor.** The active lower bracket is "the only lower bracket a rebalance *buys*" ([Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket)) — it is never sold to make room for a duration match the way a retained lower bracket may be. The over-allocated trigger is therefore **not** "`activeWeight` solves negative" (literally below zero) but "`activeWeight` solves below the active bracket's own currently-held excess share" — its floor. A retained lower bracket large and/or short-duration enough can otherwise pull the unconstrained solve's `activeWeight` down toward (but not past) zero without ever going negative, which the original negative-only trigger missed entirely: the active bracket's excess got treated as pure "target cost" and sold down to whatever the unconstrained weight computed, backwards from the intent (a real portfolio hit exactly this: a large, short-duration Jan-2034 retained lower bracket computed an active-bracket weight of ~0 for the newer Jan-2036 bracket, wiping its excess to 0 while 2034 kept most of its own — see 3.0 §Bracket Identification Rules for the traced case). Fixed by threading the active bracket's own currently-held excess share into `bracketWeightsN` as `activeFloorWeight` (default 0, so every caller that predates this fix — passing no floor — reproduces the exact old math when the floor genuinely is 0) and using it as the sell-trigger and the target `activeWeight` is restored to, in place of the literal 0 used throughout this section for illustration.

**Under-allocated** (feasible with all retained excess frozen at its current quantity, `activeWeight` at or above its floor):
- Freeze all retained excess at its current quantity — **never increased**.
- Buy the active lower bracket to cover the remainder — never below its own floor (its currently-held excess).

For each lower bracket `b`:
`excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(targetCost_b / costPerBond_b)`

Up to two retained maturities are currently identified from holdings and passed to `bracketWeightsN`: one cross-year orig-lower bracket (the Excess ARA pick) and, independently, any OTHER held CUSIP sharing the active lower bracket's own maturity year (3.0 §Bracket Identification Rules) — a maturity year may hold more than one TIPS (DD §Bracket Year TIPS), so the active bracket's own year is not exempt from carrying a retained holding alongside it. Identifying more than one cross-year retained generation from holdings is a known gap, tracked separately. The active lower bracket is always resolved from `tipsMarketData` by the rule, never from holdings.

**Implementation:** `runRebalance`'s summary exposes every retained maturity `bracketWeightsN` solved a weight for — cross-year and same-year alike — as `retainedLegs` (`{cusip, year, duration, weight}[]`, same order `bracketWeightsN` received them, earliest maturity first), so `Σ retainedLegs[i].weight·duration + newLowerWeight3·newLowerDuration + upperWeight·upperDuration = dGap` and the four quantities' weights sum to 1 can both be verified directly from the summary. `origLowerWeight` (the first retained maturity only) predates this and stays for its existing callers; a ladder with a same-year retained maturity too has a weight that `origLowerWeight` alone cannot account for. (Added 2026-09-30: a real-holdings test summing `origLowerWeight + newLowerWeight3 + upperWeight` for a ladder with both a cross-year AND a same-year retained maturity got 0.84 instead of 1 — the summary had no way to expose the second maturity's weight at all, not an engine defect; the engine's own internal weights already summed to 1 exactly.)

**User control — "Retain lower bracket excess"** (Rebalance only; on by default). When **off**, retained excess is sold outright and the bracket role consolidates into the active lower bracket; those maturities fall into the normal rebalance sweep and are rebuilt to funded-year quantity only. When **on**, the priority rule above applies. The toggle governs the **excess** only — funded-year holdings in those maturities are unaffected either way.

**Round-Trip Rounding Note.** The over-allocated partial-sell target computed above is a continuous dollar figure; sizing it to a real, tradeable holding rounds it to a whole number of bonds (`excessQtyAfter_b = ROUND(...)`, above). When that target isn't an exact multiple of one bond's cost, the rounded holding can land a few dollars short of the exact target. Exporting and reimporting that (now-rounded) portfolio, unchanged, can therefore surface a **very small residual trade** on the next rebalance — up to about one bond, bought into the active lower bracket (never back into retained excess, which is never bought) to cover the shortfall the rounding left behind. This is an ordinary consequence of investing in whole bond lots, not a bug in the priority rule: it is accepted rather than engineered away, and the test suite tolerates it (≤1 bond of churn, cash within one bond's cost) rather than asserting bit-perfect round-trip idempotency in this specific regime. It does not occur in the under-allocated regime (retained frozen exactly at its current, already-whole-bond quantity — nothing to round) or when no retained maturity is held.

### Future 30Y Cover Pair
When `lastYear` exceeds the longest issued maturity year, two outstanding TIPS duration-match the synthetic Future 30Y rungs:
- **Lower cover**: the outstanding TIPS with the longest modified duration at or below that of the Synthetic TIPS of the first Future 30Y year alone, measured at LOWER_COVER_YIELD (DD §5.0, §5.1). That is the lowest target any ladder can have, so the lower cover lies at or below the target of every ladder, and a fixed yield above the market yield keeps the choice from changing with each day's yield ([research](../research/Future_30Y_Cover_Excess_Analysis.md)).
- **Upper cover**: the outstanding TIPS with the longest modified duration, excluding the lower cover.

Longest duration, not longest maturity: no outstanding TIPS matures after the Future 30Y rungs, so only a coupon far below current yields gives a duration high enough. Such a TIPS behaves close to a zero-coupon security, and its duration can exceed that of a later maturity. A TIPS with a very low coupon against current yields can have a longer duration than a TIPS maturing years later, so which maturity is a cover changes with the market (DD §5.1).

### Excess Quantities
For each bracket `b`:
```
excessQty_b = ROUND(totalBlockCost × weight_b / costPerBond_b)
```
where `costPerBond_b = price/100 × indexRatio × 1000` (per **TIPS Basics** — IR always included). This formula always applies to the upper bracket (2040), and to the lower brackets whenever no retained bracket excess is present.

**Exception — retained bracket excess:** when lower bracket excess exists in maturities older than the active lower bracket, the priority rule (see §Retained Bracket Excess) replaces the weight formula across the lower brackets. The upper bracket (2040) still uses the standard formula above.

### Amount of a Gap Year and a Future 30Y Year
The **Amount** of a gap year or a Future 30Y year is shown on that year's row, as a funded year's Amount is shown on its row. It is what the year's TIPS and [LMI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#lmi) deliver in that year, so it comes to about the year's DARA, apart from the rounding of its quantity to a whole number of TIPS. Build and Rebalance both show it, before and after.
- **Gap year**: the P+I of its [Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-10y-tips) plus the LMI that sized its quantity down (the interest of the later gap years and the pre-ladder credit). By the year it matures all of the later gap years have been issued, so that interest is their full annual coupons, the figure [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) uses as LMI. The Amount therefore equals the Amount Pass 1 gives its Synthetic TIPS.
- **Future 30Y year**: the P+I of its [Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#simulated-outstanding-future-30y-tips) plus the interest of the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS of the later Future 30Y years. By the year it matures all of them have been issued, so that interest is their full annual coupons, the figure [Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) uses as LMI. The Amount therefore equals the Amount Pass 1 gives its Synthetic TIPS.

The excess rows show no Amount. What matters on them is the cost of the excess, which duration matching sets (§Duration Matching), so their popups show how that cost is reached.

## Plain-Language Walkthrough: Gap Coverage as Two Passes

The gap and bracket rules above (§Gap Years, §Gap Year Coverage Model, §Duration Matching) can be
read as two passes over the ladder. This section restates them in plain language for orientation; the
formulas above remain the single source of truth — this section adds no new math of its own.

**[Pass 1](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-1) — size the synthetic TIPS, longest to shortest.** Synthetic TIPS stand in for the gap
years (§Gap Years Steps 1–7). Each gap year's synthetic quantity is sized from its own DARA minus
incoming LMI — the same subtraction any funded year uses — but the incoming LMI here is built from
two sources only: the actual TIPS above the gap years (`actualTIPSLMI`), and the running total of
synthetic coupon already sized for the longer gap years already processed in this same pass
(`runningSynLMI` — the coupon on the Synthetic TIPS of the last gap year feeds into the sizing of the gap year before it, and so on down the gap years). For the gap years, its output is the average duration of the gap years and the gap
total cost (§Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted). The Excess TIPS pays no interest in a gap year, because it is sold by the last conversion (§Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS), so the incoming LMI has no third source.

**Between the passes — duration-match the excess.** The average duration of the synthetic TIPS from
Pass 1 (cost-weighted, §Average Gap Year Duration is Cost-Weighted) is the target the real bracket
year holdings must match. Solving the weight equations (§2-Bracket Weights (Standard), or §Retained
Bracket Excess (Rebalance)'s `bracketWeightsN` when older retained maturities are frozen at their own
duration) allocates the gap total cost across the bracket years so that the cost-weighted mean of
their durations equals the average duration of the gap years, and this determines each bracket year's
excess TIPS **quantity** (§Excess Quantities). The Future 30Y cover years are matched the same way
against their own years and total cost (§Future 30Y Cover Pair). The Gap Dur popup shows Pass 1's
per-year synthetic sizing with the ladder's own live DARA and duration figures; the Cost popup on an
excess row shows this weight solve and the resulting quantity.

**[Pass 2](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#pass-2) — build the real ladder, longest to shortest.** With the excess quantities now known, the
standard longest-to-shortest sweep (§Algorithm (Longest-to-Shortest Sweep)) runs over the *actual*
holdings. The Excess TIPS is sold before it matures, so it contributes no interest to its maturity year (DD Same-Maturity Excess Interest). Its interest, with that of the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS and the Net Cash, is credited to the funded years before the first gap year (§Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS, §Interest credited to a funded year).

**Why a rebalance is needed later.** Pass 1's synthetic coupon rate is an interpolated guess (§Gap
Years Steps 2–3) — the real gap-year TIPS won't exist, and its actual coupon won't be known, until
Treasury issues it. Each rebalance after a gap-year TIPS is issued corrects for this: the newly
real TIPS replaces its synthetic stand-in, and the excess TIPS are resized against the now-current
run of remaining gap years.

Nothing about that transition is configured or scheduled. The gap set is derived from the market
data on every run (`getGapYears`), so a gap year stops being one on the day its TIPS becomes
tradable, and the [Active Lower Bracket](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#active-lower-bracket)
advances to it by the same rule. What the holder sees on that first rebalance is:

- **The new maturity is bought**, both its funded year rung and, in 2-bracket, its share of the lower
  bracket excess for the gap years that remain.
- **The previous lower bracket is sold down.** In 2-bracket its entire excess goes, since the bracket
  role has moved; in Multi-bracket it is retained and trimmed only as the gap years shrink
  (§Retained Bracket Excess). Multi-bracket trades roughly half as many TIPS for a slightly larger
  net purchase.
- **The upper bracket is trimmed**, the gap years now spanning one fewer year.
- **Small changes elsewhere**, from the real coupon differing from the interpolated synthetic one and
  from the curve having moved since the ladder was built.

A rebalance run partway through a calendar year will also show a buy in the current rung for an
unrelated reason (§Settlement-year LMI is remaining coupons only). Inflation contributes nothing,
provided DARA is carried in its own basis (3.0 §DARA Reference Date).

## Algorithm (Longest-to-Shortest Sweep)

Process actual bonds and funded years **longest to shortest**.

1. **Initialize LMI Pool**: `runningLMI = 0`.
2. **For each year Y (longest to shortest)**:
   a. **Incoming LMI**: Sum of inflation-adjusted `annualInterest` from all bonds maturing *after* Y.
   b. **Credited interest**: The amounts credited to year Y for the Simulated Outstanding Future 10Y TIPS, the Simulated Outstanding Future 30Y TIPS, the Excess TIPS, the Cover Excess and the Net Cash (§Gap Year Simulated Outstanding TIPS, §Future 30Y Simulated Outstanding TIPS). They are worked out for year Y directly. The Excess TIPS and the Cover Excess are sold before they mature, so none contributes interest to its maturity year.
   c. **Target FY Quantity**:
      - `Required_PI = YearDARA - IncomingLMI - creditedInterest_Y`
      - `fundedYearQty_Y = MAX(0, ROUND(Required_PI / piPerBond_Y))`
   d. **Update LMI Pool**:
      - `runningLMI += fundedYearQty_Y × annualInterestPerBond_Y`
3. **Gap Years**: Contribute zero to the `runningLMI` pool (no actual bonds held; their role is played by bracket excess in other years).

## Transparency & UI Requirements

Duration matching must be educational and traceable. The UI must provide:
1. **Individual Disclosure**: Every synthetic/hypothetical bond used to calculate the average must be listed with its own Modified Duration.
2. **Derivation**: The "Average Duration" must be explicitly shown as the cost-weighted mean of the listed bonds (weighted by each rung's cost = qty × 1000).
3. **Fulcrum Visualization**: A visual representation showing the bracket durations flanking the average duration.
4. **Cost-to-Quantity Chain**: A breakdown showing how the gap years' total cost is weighted and then converted to discrete bond units.
