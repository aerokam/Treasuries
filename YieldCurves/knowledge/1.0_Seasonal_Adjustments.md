# 1.0 Seasonal Adjustments (SA)

## Overview
Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) yields are calculated based on the "**[Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price)**", which is Canty's term for the unadjusted price; this price does not include the inflation adjustment component. However, since TIPS are indexed to the Non-Seasonally Adjusted CPI (CPI-NSA), their monthly performance is subject to predictable seasonal patterns (e.g., higher inflation in spring, lower in autumn).

The **[Seasonal Adjustment (SA)](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor)** removes that predictable pattern, so real yields can be compared across maturity months on the same basis the market already prices them on.

---

## Explaining Seasonality

### Level 1: Common Sense
Prices for things like gas, clothing, and food go up and down with the seasons (e.g., higher demand for gas in summer). This "noise" makes it look like inflation is moving faster or slower than it really is. We "fix" the math by smoothing out these seasonal waves so you can see the real value of the TIPS without being fooled by the time of year.

### Level 2: The Individual Investor's Perspective
An individual comparing TIPS wants to know which is the better value. A raw yield in April can read higher than one in October only because inflation is seasonally higher in the first half of the year than the second, not because the April bond is a better deal. Removing the predictable, recurring seasonal pattern lets bonds across the curve be compared on the same basis.

### Level 3: Institutional Logic
Institutional investors set TIPS prices, and therefore TIPS yields. They price the seasonal pattern in directly, valuing a bond higher when its indexation period covers seasonally high inflation and lower when it covers seasonally low inflation. The seasonal adjustment removes that same calendar-driven component, using the same seasonal factors the market does.

### Level 4: The Math
The seasonal factor for a date is the ratio of two [Reference CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi) values for that date:

\[ S_t = \frac{RefCPI_{NSA}(t)}{RefCPI_{SA}(t)} \]

- \( RefCPI_{NSA}(t) \): the published Ref CPI, the non-seasonally adjusted series that drives TIPS inflation accrual.
- \( RefCPI_{SA}(t) \): a seasonally adjusted Ref CPI, constructed the same way from BLS's seasonally adjusted CPI-U.

To adjust a TIPS, multiply its [clean price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price) by \( S_{Settle} / S_{Maturity} \) and recompute the yield from the adjusted price. The full statement is [The Transformation](#the-transformation) below.

---

## The Transformation

The seasonal adjustment is Canty (2009) Equation 14, the working approximation the apps use ([Canty.md](Canty.md) §4):

\[ SACP \approx CP \times \frac{S_{Settle}}{S_{Maturity}} \]

where \( CP \), \( SACP \), and \( S_t \) map from Canty's general inflation-linked-bond notation to these TIPS quantities ([Canty.md](Canty.md) §1a has the full table):

| Canty | TIPS term | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \( CP \) | [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price) | the quoted real price, before inflation adjustment |
| \( SACP \) | [Seasonally Adjusted Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sacp) | the clean price with the predictable seasonal component removed |
| \( S_t \) | [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) on date \( t \) | \( RefCPI_{NSA}(t) / RefCPI_{SA}(t) \) (Level 4) |
| \( S_{Settle} / S_{Maturity} \) | [SA Price Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-price-factor) | the multiplier applied to the clean price |

In app terms: `SACP ≈ CP × SA_Price_Factor`, where `SA_Price_Factor = SA_Factor(settlement date) / SA_Factor(maturity date)`. `CP` is whichever quoted price is in use; the formula does not depend on the choice (see [Data Sources](#data-sources)).

Because the seasonal pattern repeats each year, \( S_t \) depends only on the calendar position of \( t \) — its month and day, not the year. So \( S_{Maturity} \) is fixed by the month and day of the maturity date alone.

When settlement and maturity fall on the same month and day (a whole number of years apart), \( S_{Settle} \) and \( S_{Maturity} \) are equal, the SA Price Factor is 1.0, and `SACP = CP`, so there is no adjustment. It departs from 1.0 only by the seasonal difference over the leftover partial year ([Canty.md](Canty.md) §4, the anniversary identity).

### The SA Yield

The **[SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield)** is the ordinary yield-to-maturity of `SACP` (Actual/Actual day count, semi-annual compounding). Seasonally adjusted breakeven inflation is the nominal yield minus the SA Yield.

### Getting the two SA Factors

Both come from `RefCpiNsaSa.csv` via `shared/src/ref-cpi.js#saFactorForDate`.

- **Settlement date**: a known recent date, so its SA Factor is that date's exact daily value.
- **Maturity date**: only the month and day matter (above). The maturity date of nearly every outstanding TIPS lies beyond the published series, so its SA Factor is the same month and day taken from the most recent completed cycle, scaled by a horizon confidence weight ([Horizon-Dependent Maturity Factor](#horizon-dependent-maturity-factor)).

<a id="bls-seasonal-factor-estimation"></a>
## How BLS Derives the Seasonal Factors

The seasonal factor series is not a fixed property of the calendar. BLS re-estimates it every year from a moving window of recent data, with a method that treats seasonality as something that evolves.

- **Method.** CPI-U seasonal adjustment uses X-13ARIMA-SEATS (X-12-ARIMA before 2018, X-11 before that). It divides the series by a centred moving average to isolate the seasonal-plus-irregular component, then smooths that component across years, separately for each calendar month, to extract the seasonal factor. An ARIMA model extends the series at both ends so the moving averages reach the most recent months.
- **Moving seasonal filters.** The smoothing applied to each month's values is a moving average spanning a few years: a 3×3 filter covers about 5 years, 3×5 about 7 years, 3×9 about 11 years. BLS selects the filter per series. A year outside the window contributes nothing to the current factor.
- **Annual re-estimation.** With the January release each February, BLS re-runs seasonal adjustment for every series and revises the prior five years of seasonally adjusted data. Older data is frozen at whatever factors were current when it left the window.
- **Projected factors.** BLS publishes seasonal factors for the coming year only, from the asymmetric end filters and the ARIMA extension, and applies them to incoming months until the next annual re-estimation.
- **Aggregation.** The all-items index is seasonally adjusted by summing roughly 70 separately adjusted component indexes; a component that fails BLS's seasonality test enters the aggregate unadjusted.

The method produces a seasonal factor for the recent past and one year forward, and no basis for projecting one decades ahead. Canty's assumption that the pattern repeats unchanged every year ([The Transformation](#the-transformation)) is a working approximation whose error grows with distance from the estimation window. [1.1 Seasonal Factor Drift](1.1_Seasonal_Factor_Drift.md) measures that error and its effect on long-dated TIPS.

<a id="horizon-dependent-maturity-factor"></a>
## Horizon-Dependent Maturity Factor

Because the maturity SA Factor of nearly every outstanding TIPS is the current seasonal pattern projected onto a future date, and that projection decays with horizon ([above](#bls-seasonal-factor-estimation)), the seasonal adjustment scales the projected departure from 1.0 by a confidence weight:

\[ S_{Maturity}^{eff} = 1 + (S_{Maturity} - 1)\, w(h) \]

- \( h \) — years from the current date to the maturity date.
- \( w(h) = A^2 / (A^2 + \sigma_{drift}(h)^2) \) — the signal-to-noise ratio of the seasonal amplitude \( A \) against the drift \( \sigma_{drift}(h) \) measured for that maturity's calendar month in [1.1 §4–§5](1.1_Seasonal_Factor_Drift.md). Both quantities are measured, not fitted.
- \( w(h) = 1 \) for \( h \le 2 \): within roughly the horizon BLS itself projects factors, the projection error is immaterial, and the front-end adjustment is both the largest and the only part corroborated by the maturity-month cross-section in the market ([2.2 §2](2.2_SAO_Residual_Analysis.md), [1.1 §7.1](1.1_Seasonal_Factor_Drift.md)).
- Beyond \( h = 2 \) the weight follows the signal-to-noise form. For February 15, the maturity date of every TIPS maturing 2040 or later, \( w \) reaches about 0.45 at 30 years.

\( S_{Settle} \) is never scaled: it is a measured recent value, not a projection.

The long-end `SA − ask` adjustment, about −4 bp at the 2050s maturities under the unscaled projection, becomes about −2 to −3 bp; the front end is unchanged ([1.1 §7](1.1_Seasonal_Factor_Drift.md)).

## Data Sources
- **Prices**: Sourced from FedInvest (daily) or uploaded via Broker CSV (Schwab/Fidelity).
  - **Broker**: Provides **Ask Prices** (actionable buy prices).
  - **FedInvest**: Provides **Mid-Market Prices** (midpoint of bid and ask). Because mid-market prices are lower than ask prices, FedInvest yields will appear higher than broker ask yields.
- **SA Factors**: `RefCpiNsaSa.csv` in R2, one row per calendar day holding `Ref CPI NSA`, `Ref CPI SA`, and their ratio (the SA Factor).
  - `Ref CPI SA` applies the 31 CFR Appendix B daily interpolation, the same interpolation used for the Ref CPI, to BLS's monthly seasonally adjusted CPI-U.
  - BLS publishes the seasonally adjusted CPI-U only monthly, and Appendix B officially interpolates the non-seasonally adjusted series alone. A daily seasonally adjusted Ref CPI has no official form: it is a constructed series, built for seasonal yield comparison, always calculated and never retrieved because there is nothing official to retrieve. See [DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi).
  - The interpolation is defined once in `shared/src/ref-cpi.js` and imported wherever it is needed.
