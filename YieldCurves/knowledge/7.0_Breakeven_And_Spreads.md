# 7.0 Breakeven Inflation and Bid-Ask Spreads

**Specifies:** [3.5 Compute breakeven inflation](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL2_YIELDCURVES), [3.6 Compute bid and ask spreads](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL2_YIELDCURVES)  
**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js`

The two are specified together because neither produces a series of its own from a source: each takes yields another process already produced and subtracts one from another. What distinguishes them is which two.

<a id="breakeven-inflation"></a>
## 1.0 Breakeven Inflation (3.5)

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#processAndRenderBei`, `YieldCurves/src/app.js#findClosestNominal`

Subtracts a real yield from a nominal yield. The app produces two of them, by two different methods, and they do not agree.

### 1.1 Per security

For each TIPS, the nominal Treasury whose maturity date is nearest to that TIPS is selected, and the breakeven is that nominal's yield less the TIPS yield. Three are produced per TIPS, from the [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield) on the [ask](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) side, the [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), and the [SAO Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yield).

**Nearest is unconditional.** The selection takes the smallest absolute difference in maturity date with no limit on how large that difference may be, so a TIPS with no nominal maturing near it is still paired, and its breakeven then compares two different terms. This is a property of the method, not a data problem, and it is why the two methods disagree most where nominal maturities are sparse.

### 1.2 Across terms

The fitted nominal curve less the fitted TIPS curve, read at the same term, on a half-year grid over the range where both fits are valid.

Both inputs come from [4.0](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md). The nominal fit is restricted to Notes and Bonds; the TIPS fit uses the seasonally adjusted prices, so this breakeven is a seasonally adjusted one throughout.

This method has no pairing to go wrong: both yields are read at the same term by construction. Where the two methods disagree, [1.1](#breakeven-inflation) carries the approximation.

**Output:** the per-security breakevens and the curve breakeven, to [6.0](./6.0_Rendering.md).

## 2.0 Bid and Ask Spreads (3.6)

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#buildProcessedTipsBonds`, `YieldCurves/src/app.js#renderNominalsTable`

Subtracts the [ask](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) side from the [bid](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#bid) side, in yield and in price, for each security quoted with both.

Only the market quotes carry both sides, so this process has one source. Securities priced from [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) alone produce no spread.

### 2.1 Yield spread

Bid yield less ask yield, in basis points.

For TIPS both sides are calculated from price in [5.0 §2.7](./5.0_Load_And_Parse.md#build-priced-bonds) rather than read from the quote, so the two sides share one convention and the difference between them is a spread. Reading the quoted bid yield and calculating the ask yield would put a method difference into the result alongside the spread.

### 2.2 Price spread

Ask price less bid price, as a percentage of the ask price.

**TIPS use the index-ratio adjusted prices and nominal Treasuries use the quoted prices.** The adjusted price is what is actually paid for a TIPS, so the spread is stated on the amount that changes hands rather than on the real price. For a nominal Treasury the two are the same figure, so no adjustment arises.

**Output:** the yield and price spreads per security, to [6.0](./6.0_Rendering.md).

## 3.0 What Neither Process Does

Neither reads a data store, and neither calculates a yield. Every input is a yield or a price produced upstream, and each process performs one subtraction on inputs that are already on a common basis. A result that looks wrong is a defect in the input or in the pairing, not in the arithmetic here.
