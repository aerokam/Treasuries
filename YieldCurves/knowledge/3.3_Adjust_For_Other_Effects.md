# 3.3 Adjust for other effects

**Specifies:** [3.3 Adjust for other effects](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL2_YIELDCURVES)  
**Implemented by:** `shared/src/spot-curve.js`  
**Derived from:** [Canty (2009)](./Canty.md)  
**Evidence:** [The SAO Residual](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)  
**Input:** [SA Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yields), from [3.2](./3.2_Adjust_For_Seasonality.md)  
**Output:** [SAO Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yields), to [3.5](./3.5_Calculate_Breakeven_Inflation.md) and [3.7](./3.7_Render_Charts_And_Tables.md)

## Overview
The standard seasonal adjustment (SA) transform (market price → SA price → SA Yield) removes predictable, recurring CPI seasonality. The resulting SA real-yield curve still has "wiggles" — points above or below a smooth curve. The **SAO** step removes those wiggles by fitting the SA yields to a **smooth fair-value curve** and reading each TIPS off it.

The **O** is **other**. Seasonality is one identified, named cause of deviation from fair value, and the SA transform removes it by construction. Everything else that moves a TIPS off a smooth curve is removed here, as a class: institutional participants act on relative-value factors this project cannot enumerate, and the step is built to remove all of them rather than a named list of known events.

## Inspired by Canty's outlier analysis, but operationally a curve smoother
Our SAO step is **inspired by Canty's outlier-factor analysis** ([Canty.md](Canty.md), Eq 20–21), in which the inflation index decomposes as `I_t = T_t · S_t · O_t` and the outlier index `O_t` captures **non-seasonal** effects on top of trend and seasonality. Canty determines `O_t` **analytically, per known event** — a VAT hike, a gasoline move since the last CPI release — i.e. specific, identifiable, non-seasonal inflation shocks.

We are **not able to identify specific outlier factors** that way. So instead of constructing an `O_t`, we **simply smooth the curve**, on the working assumption that the deviation of a TIPS from a smooth curve is driven by factors **not particularly relevant to the retail buy-and-hold investor, as far as we know** (e.g. liquidity or other relative-value effects, to which such a holder is indifferent). In short: Canty isolates known outlier factors and adjusts for them; we cannot isolate them, so we smooth them away — but the *motivation* (strip non-seasonal noise that distorts comparison across the curve) is the same. **This is not a reimplementation of Canty's `O_t`; do not conflate the two.**

## Why snap everything to the curve
For a buy-and-hold retail holder, a point away from a smooth curve only matters if a **value-relevant** factor explains it. The candidate factors were tested empirically in [SAO Residual Analysis](SAO_Residual_Analysis.md) and found immaterial:
- **Coupon and index ratio** — the cleanest evidence is **same-maturity-date pairs**: two TIPS maturing on the same date share the same seasonal factor, so any yield gap is pure coupon + index ratio. Across such pairs the coupon differs ~3.5× and the index ratio spans e.g. 1.35 vs 1.58, yet the SA yields differ by **~1 bp at most** (typically). That is strong evidence neither materially affects yield.
- Remaining deviations are structured by **maturity month** (a deseasonalization residual) or are **idiosyncratic** (e.g. a liquidity-driven richness). Neither changes fair value to a buy-and-hold holder — a liquidity cheapness just makes a bond "a better deal," not a different fair value.

So **every deviation that isn't value-relevant is smoothed out** — but only where the underlying residual is actually present. See [The calculation](#the-calculation) below.

[SAO Residual Analysis §2](SAO_Residual_Analysis.md#2-the-pattern-a-maturity-month-residual) found the residual "amortizes with maturity — large at 2027, sub-1bp by 2031" within its 2027–2031 study region. Extending that measurement to the full curve ([SAO Residual Analysis §6](SAO_Residual_Analysis.md#6-extension-does-the-residual-persist-at-the-long-end)) confirms it: deviation from a full-curve NSS fit runs 5–15bp in the first maturity year, decays through the mid curve, and is flat noise (~1-2bp, no maturity-month sign pattern) from roughly **5 years to maturity onward**. Snapping those long-end points to a single global NSS curve anyway would smooth away real coupon/relative-value structure that §3 already established should *not* be smoothed — the opposite of the intent. That is why the calculation below weights the snap by years-to-maturity instead of applying it uniformly.

## Caveat: we may be smoothing away real expected-return differences
This smoothing is an **assumption, not a certainty.** We do not actually know that an apparently-artificial high yield *is* artificial. It may genuinely deliver a higher return to whoever buys it. The parallel is instructive: the SA step is really an adjustment to **expected nominal return** — it levels out the portion of return that is *seasonally predictable* so bonds maturing in different months can be compared fairly. By the same logic, we **suspect there are other factors that also affect expected nominal return**, but in ways that are **not seasonally predictable** and that we cannot model. If a deviation reflects one of those, smoothing it down hides a genuinely better (or worse) deal rather than removing noise. We accept that risk because, for the target holder, we have no identified value-relevant factor to justify keeping any given point off the curve — but the uncertainty is real and is the main known limitation of this approach.

## The calculation

<a id="the-smooth-curve"></a>
The smooth curve is **Nelson-Siegel-Svensson (NSS)** — the Fed / Gürkaynak-Sack-Wright standard for fitting real-yield term structures, which guarantees a smooth, economically-shaped curve:
\[ y(\tau) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}}{\tau/\lambda_1} + \beta_2\left(\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}}{\tau/\lambda_1}-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}\right) + \beta_3\left(\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_2}}{\tau/\lambda_2}-e^{-\tau/\lambda_2}\right) \]

- Fit the curve (`fitNSS`) to every TIPS with a term of `SAO_NOISE_YRS` (0.5 years) or more, using each one's [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield) against its term to maturity `τ`.
  - Grid-search the two decay parameters `λ1, λ2`; for each pair, solve the four `β` coefficients by OLS; keep the pair with the lowest sum of squared residuals.
  - A TIPS with `τ` under `SAO_NOISE_YRS` is excluded from this fit — its SA Yield is price-noise-dominated that close to maturity (see [The very short end](#the-very-short-end)) — but every TIPS, including this one, still gets a curve value in the next step.
- Read the curve value `y(τ)` off that fitted curve for each TIPS, at its own term to maturity.
  - If `τ` is below the curve's shortest fitted term: hold the curve flat at its value at that shortest term, rather than extrapolate — the NSS form is unconstrained below its shortest anchor and diverges there (see [The very short end](#the-very-short-end)).
- Calculate a smoothing weight for the TIPS, from `τ`:
  - If `τ < SAO_NOISE_YRS` (0.5 years): weight = 1.
  - If `SAO_NOISE_YRS ≤ τ ≤ SAO_BLEND_START_YRS` (5 years): weight = 1 — a full snap to the curve.
  - If `SAO_BLEND_START_YRS < τ < SAO_BLEND_END_YRS` (6 years): weight declines linearly from 1 to 0.
  - If `τ ≥ SAO_BLEND_END_YRS` (6 years): weight = 0 — the SAO Yield below is the raw SA Yield, unsmoothed.
- Calculate the [SAO Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yield): `weight × y(τ) + (1 − weight) × SA Yield`.
- Record the deviation, `SA Yield − y(τ)`, as a rich/cheap relative-value diagnostic (shown in the drill-down), regardless of the weight. (If a value-relevant factor were ever identified even in the blend band, that — and only that — would justify further withholding smoothing.)

The constants live in `shared/src/spot-curve.js` (`SAO_NOISE_YRS`, `SAO_BLEND_START_YRS`, `SAO_BLEND_END_YRS`); the fit is `fitNSS()`, the whole calculation is `calculateSAO()`.

<a id="the-very-short-end"></a>
## The very short end
Even after seasonal adjustment, yields at the very short end can look extreme. A TIPS close to maturity has little unknown inflation left, so it trades to be competitive with nominal Treasuries, and its real yield loses much of its meaning. The SAO value read off the curve probably carries more meaning there than the SA Yield itself, but neither carries much that close to maturity. A TIPS a few weeks from maturity would otherwise read hundreds of basis points negative if the curve were extrapolated instead of held flat.
