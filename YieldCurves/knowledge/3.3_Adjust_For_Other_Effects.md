# 3.3 Adjust for other effects

**Specifies:** [3.3 Adjust for other effects](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL2_YIELDCURVES)  
**Implemented by:** `shared/src/spot-curve.js`  
**Derived from:** [Canty (2009)](./Canty.md)  
**Evidence:** [The SAO Residual](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)  
**Input:** [SA Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yields), from [Adjust for seasonality (3.2)](./3.2_Adjust_For_Seasonality.md)  
**Output:** [SAO Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yields), to [Calculate breakeven inflation (3.5)](./3.5_Calculate_Breakeven_Inflation.md) and [Render charts and tables (3.7)](./3.7_Render_Charts_And_Tables.md)

Read the [SA Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yields) and produce the [SAO Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yields): fit a smooth curve to the SA Yields, and give each TIPS a yield between its curve value and its SA Yield, weighted by its term to maturity ([The calculation](#the-calculation)).

## Overview
The standard seasonal adjustment (SA) transform (market price → SA price → [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield)) removes predictable, recurring CPI seasonality. The resulting SA real-yield curve still has points above or below a smooth curve. The **SAO** step replaces those points with the value read from a **smooth fair-value curve**.

The **O** is **other**. Seasonality is one identified, named cause of deviation from fair value, and the SA transform removes it by construction. Everything else that moves a TIPS off a smooth curve is removed here, as a class: institutional participants act on relative-value factors this project cannot enumerate, and the step removes all of them rather than a named list of known events ([SAO Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yield)).

## Relation to Canty's outlier analysis
[Canty (2009)](./Canty.md) Eq 20–21 decomposes the inflation index as `I_t = T_t · S_t · O_t`, where the outlier index `O_t` captures non-seasonal effects on top of trend and seasonality. Canty determines `O_t` analytically per known event: a VAT hike, a gasoline move since the last CPI release.

The SAO step identifies no specific outlier factor. It smooths the curve instead, on the assumption that the deviation of a TIPS from a smooth curve is driven by factors not relevant to a buy-and-hold holder, such as liquidity or other relative-value effects, to which such a holder is indifferent. Canty's `O_t` and the SAO step share a motivation, to strip non-seasonal noise that distorts comparison across the curve, and differ in method.

## Why every deviation is smoothed
For a buy-and-hold holder, a point away from a smooth curve matters only if a value-relevant factor explains it. [SAO Residual Analysis](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md) tested the candidate factors and found them immaterial:

- **Coupon and index ratio.** Two TIPS maturing on the same date share the same seasonal factor, so a yield gap between them is coupon and index ratio alone. In four same-date pairs the coupon differs by up to about 3.5 times and the index ratio by up to about 2 times, and the SA yields differ by 1.0 to 1.4 bp in three pairs and by 4.5 bp in the 2027-01 pair ([SAO Residual Analysis §3.1](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)).
- **Maturity month and idiosyncratic effects.** The remaining deviations are keyed to the maturity month (a deseasonalization residual) or are idiosyncratic, such as a liquidity-driven richness. Neither changes fair value to a buy-and-hold holder.

The residual amortizes with maturity. Within the 2027–2031 study region it is large at 2027 and under 1 bp by 2031 ([SAO Residual Analysis §2](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)). Over the full curve, at settlement 2026-07-01, the mean absolute deviation from a full-curve fit is 6.4 bp for 2027 maturities, 3.5 bp for 2028 and 0.8 to 1.9 bp for 2030 through 2036, with no maturity-month sign pattern from 2032-07 onward ([SAO Residual Analysis §6](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)). Smoothing those long-end points to a single global curve would remove coupon and relative-value structure that §3 of that analysis established is not seasonal residual. The calculation below therefore weights the smoothing by term to maturity and does not apply it uniformly.

## Caveat: real expected-return differences may be smoothed away
The smoothing is an assumption. An apparently artificial high yield may deliver a higher return to whoever buys it. The SA step adjusts expected nominal return by the portion that is seasonally predictable, so bonds maturing in different months can be compared. Other factors may also affect expected nominal return in ways that are not seasonally predictable and that the project cannot model. A deviation that reflects one of them is a better or worse deal, and smoothing hides it. The approach accepts that risk because no value-relevant factor has been identified that would justify keeping any given point off the curve. This is the main known limitation ([SAO Residual Analysis §5.1](./SAO_Residual_Analysis.md)).

## The calculation

<a id="the-smooth-curve"></a>
The smooth curve is **Nelson-Siegel-Svensson (NSS)**, the Fed and Gürkaynak-Sack-Wright standard for fitting real-yield term structures:
\[ y(\tau) = \beta_0 + \beta_1\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}}{\tau/\lambda_1} + \beta_2\left(\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}}{\tau/\lambda_1}-e^{-\tau/\lambda_1}\right) + \beta_3\left(\frac{1-e^{-\tau/\lambda_2}}{\tau/\lambda_2}-e^{-\tau/\lambda_2}\right) \]

- Fit the curve (`fitNSS`) to every TIPS with a term of `SAO_NOISE_YRS` (0.5 years) or more, using each one's [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield) against its term to maturity `τ`.
  - Grid-search the two decay parameters `λ1, λ2`; for each pair, solve the four `β` coefficients by OLS; keep the pair with the lowest sum of squared residuals.
  - A TIPS with no [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield) is excluded from this fit and has no SAO Yield.
  - A TIPS with `τ` under `SAO_NOISE_YRS` is excluded from this fit, its SA Yield being dominated by price noise that close to maturity ([The very short end](#the-very-short-end)). Every TIPS, including this one, still gets a curve value in the next step.
- Read the curve value `y(τ)` off the fitted curve for each TIPS, at its term to maturity.
  - If `τ` is below the curve's shortest fitted term: hold the curve flat at its value at that shortest term. The NSS form is unconstrained below its shortest anchor and diverges there ([The very short end](#the-very-short-end)).
- Calculate a smoothing weight for the TIPS, from `τ` ([SAO Blend Weight](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-blend-weight)):
  - If `τ < SAO_NOISE_YRS` (0.5 years): weight = 1.
  - If `SAO_NOISE_YRS ≤ τ ≤ SAO_BLEND_START_YRS` (5 years): weight = 1.
  - If `SAO_BLEND_START_YRS < τ < SAO_BLEND_END_YRS` (6 years): weight declines linearly from 1 to 0.
  - If `τ ≥ SAO_BLEND_END_YRS`: weight = 0, and the SAO Yield below is the SA Yield.
- Calculate the [SAO Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yield): `weight × y(τ) + (1 − weight) × SA Yield`.
- Record the deviation, `SA Yield − y(τ)`, as a rich/cheap relative-value diagnostic shown in the drill-down, whatever the weight.

The constants are `SAO_NOISE_YRS`, `SAO_BLEND_START_YRS` and `SAO_BLEND_END_YRS` in `shared/src/spot-curve.js`; the fit is `fitNSS()` and the whole calculation is `calculateSAO()`.

<a id="the-very-short-end"></a>
## The very short end
Even after seasonal adjustment, yields at the very short end can look extreme. A TIPS close to maturity has little unknown inflation left, so it trades to be competitive with nominal Treasuries, and its real yield loses much of its meaning. The curve value probably has more meaning there than the SA Yield, though neither has much that close to maturity. Extrapolating the curve instead would put a TIPS a few weeks from maturity hundreds of basis points negative.
