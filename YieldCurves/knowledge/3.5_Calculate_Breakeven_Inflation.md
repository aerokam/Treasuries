# 3.5 Calculate breakeven inflation

**Specifies:** [3.5 Calculate breakeven inflation](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL2_YIELDCURVES)  
**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#processAndRenderBei`, `shared/src/breakeven.js#findClosestNominal`  
**Input:** [SAO Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yields), from [3.3](./3.3_Adjust_For_Other_Effects.md); [Treasury Yields](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#treasury-yields), from [3.1](./3.1_Parse_Sources_And_Calculate_Yields.md); [Spot Yield Curves](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#spot-yield-curves), from [3.4](./3.4_Fit_Spot_Yield_Curves.md)  
**Output:** [Breakeven Inflation](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#breakeven-inflation), to [3.7](./3.7_Render_Charts_And_Tables.md)

Subtracts a real yield from a nominal yield. The app produces two of them, by two different methods, and they do not agree.

Neither this process nor [3.6](./3.6_Calculate_Bid_And_Ask_Spreads.md) reads a data store or calculates a yield. Every input is a yield or a price produced upstream, and each performs one subtraction on inputs already on a common basis. A result that looks wrong is a defect in the input or in the pairing, not in the arithmetic here.

## Per security

For each TIPS, the nominal Treasury whose maturity date is nearest to that TIPS is selected, and the breakeven is that nominal's yield less the TIPS yield. Three are produced per TIPS, from the [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield) on the [ask](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) side, the [SA Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-yield), and the [SAO Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sao-yield).

**Nearest is unconditional.** The selection takes the smallest absolute difference in maturity date with no limit on how large that difference may be, so a TIPS with no nominal maturing near it is still paired, and its breakeven then compares two different terms. This is a property of the method, not a data problem, and it is why the two methods disagree most where nominal maturities are sparse.

The same selection states the nominal in every [S14](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s14) row, from one implementation, so a published breakeven and the one the app shows are stated against the same security.

## Across terms

The fitted nominal curve less the fitted TIPS curve, read at the same term, on a half-year grid over the range where both fits are valid.

Both inputs come from [3.4](./3.4_Fit_Spot_Yield_Curves.md). The nominal fit is restricted to Notes and Bonds; the TIPS fit uses the seasonally adjusted prices, so this breakeven is a seasonally adjusted one throughout.

This method has no pairing to go wrong: both yields are read at the same term by construction. Where the two methods disagree, the per-security method carries the approximation.

**Output:** the per-security breakevens and the curve breakeven, to [3.7](./3.7_Render_Charts_And_Tables.md).
