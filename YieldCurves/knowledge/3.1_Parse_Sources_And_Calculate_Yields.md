# 3.1 Load and Parse Source Data

**Specifies:** [3.1 Load and parse source data](/knowledge/DFD_LEVEL3_YC_LOAD) and the seven processes it decomposes into  
**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js`  
**Reference:** [FedInvest Pricing Logic](./FedInvest_Pricing_Logic.md), [FedInvest Treasury Pricing Methodology](./FedInvest_Treasury_Pricing_Methodology.md)

- [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) — [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield), [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price), [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date), [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio), [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor), [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi)

- [3.2 Seasonal Adjustments](./3.2_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) and [3.4 Spot Yield Curves](./3.4_Spot_Yield_Curves.md) — the processes downstream of this one

## Scope

Yield Curves reads five data stores and produces one set of securities that the rest of the app works from. Every process below runs in the browser at page load, and none of them writes a data store.

**Every yield in the app is calculated from price.** [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) carries a [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield) per security, calculated from price when the file was written by ingestion process 1.1, and [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) carries the [ask](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) yield its source quotes. The app uses neither. Each security is priced again against its own source's [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date), in the process that assembles its security object: the nominal Treasuries quoted in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) in [3.1.2](#parse-market-quotes), every TIPS in [3.1.7](#calculate-tips-yields), and the nominal Treasuries from [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) during rendering, in [3.7](./3.7_Rendering.md). One method across both sources, both security types and both sides of a quote is what makes the four sets comparable with each other, and a difference between two of them a difference in the market rather than in the arithmetic.

## Processes

<a id="parse-fedinvest-prices"></a>
### 3.1.1 Parse FedInvest prices

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseFedInvestPrices`

Reads [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) and splits it into the TIPS and the nominal securities, by its `Type` column. That split is what lets one store supply both the real curve and the nominal curve.

[S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) states one [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) for the whole file rather than one per security, so that date is read once and attached to every security taken from it. See [3.1.6](#determine-settlement-dates) for what that date means for this source.

**Output:** the securities in [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1), each with its [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price), its [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield), and that settlement date.

<a id="parse-market-quotes"></a>
### 3.1.2 Parse market quotes

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseMarketQuotes`, `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseFidelityNominals`, `shared/src/fidelity-parse.js#parseFidelityNominalRows`, `shared/src/fidelity-parse.js#parseFidelityTipsRows`

Reads [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) and separates its Treasury rows from its TIPS rows, by its `Product` column, taking the [bid](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) and [ask](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ask) [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price) for each.

The column names are the export's own rather than the Data Dictionary's. They are recorded as synonyms in the [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) entry, which gives the defined term each one carries.

[S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) carries the date it was downloaded on a trailing line rather than in a column. That date is read here and passed to [3.1.6](#determine-settlement-dates), which turns it into the settlement date the quoted prices are stated at.

**The nominal Treasuries are priced here.** Each one's ask yield and bid yield are calculated from its two quoted prices at that settlement date. Three properties of the quote decide that against reading the yield it states:

- The quoted price states more decimal places than the quoted yield.
- A yield is quoted on the ask side only, so the bid side is calculated in either case.
- Calculating both sides puts them on one convention, which is what makes their difference the spread of [3.6](./3.6_Bid_And_Ask_Spreads.md).

All three hold for the TIPS quoted in the same file, which [3.1.7](#calculate-tips-yields) prices on the same reasoning.

The quoted ask yield is still read, as a presence test: a row without one carries no live offer. A Treasury row is also dropped when its ask yield cannot be calculated, for want of an ask price or for a maturity date on or before the settlement date. A row with no quoted bid price keeps its ask yield and has no bid yield.

The row parse and both yield calculations live in `shared/src/fidelity-parse.js#parseFidelityNominalRows`, which the acquisition script that writes [S13](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s13) and [S15](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s15) imports as well. What the app supplies is its own gating and the [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) type vocabulary the rest of it filters on.

**STRIPS are Treasury rows.** [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) separates only Treasury from TIPS in its `Product` column, so the zero-coupon STRIPS arrive among the nominal Treasuries and are told apart by their CUSIP root. They are priced from the quoted price like every other row: at a coupon of zero the pricing routine builds no coupon cash flows, so a STRIP gets the semi-annual bond-equivalent yield of its own price, and one maturing inside half a year gets Treasury's bill investment-rate formula. The processes that fit a coupon-bond curve leave STRIPS out of the fitting set.

The TIPS rows carry their prices from here and nothing else. [3.1.7](#calculate-tips-yields) prices them, alongside the TIPS from [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1).

**Output:** the nominal Treasuries in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) with their bid and ask prices, their calculated bid and ask yields and their settlement date; the TIPS in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) with their bid and ask prices; and the date the file was downloaded.

<a id="parse-ref-cpi-and-sa-factors"></a>
### 3.1.3 Parse Ref CPI and SA factors

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseRefCpiAndSaFactors`, `shared/src/csv.js#parseCsv`

Reads [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4), giving one [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi) and one [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) per calendar day.

Lookup is by exact date. [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4) carries a row for every calendar day it covers, so a date inside its range always matches exactly and a date outside it has no value at all. Nothing falls back to a nearby date.

**Output:** the daily Ref CPI series, and the SA factors that [3.1.7](#calculate-tips-yields) and [3.2 Seasonal Adjustments](./3.2_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) apply.

<a id="parse-bond-holidays"></a>
### 3.1.4 Parse bond holidays

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseBondHolidays`, `shared/src/settlement.js#parseHolidaySet`

Reads the bond holidays file and produces the set of dates the bond market is closed. [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates) is the only process that uses it, and the only reason this store is read.

**Output:** the bond market closure dates.

<a id="parse-gsw-parameters"></a>
### 3.1.5 Parse GSW parameters

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseGswParameters`

Reads [S12](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s12), which holds the Federal Reserve's own published curve parameters and the date they were fitted for.

Nothing is evaluated here. [3.4 Spot Yield Curves](./3.4_Spot_Yield_Curves.md) evaluates them, to draw the published curve against the app's own fit.

**Output:** the published curve parameters and their observation date.

<a id="determine-settlement-dates"></a>
### 3.1.6 Determine settlement dates

**Implemented by:** `YieldCurves/src/app.js#marketSettleIso`, `shared/src/settlement.js#nextBusinessDay`

Produces the [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) each source's prices are stated at. The two sources differ, and neither is derived from the other.

- **[S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1)** carries a single date and says nothing about what it means. Yields calculated at that date, rather than at a date offset from it, are the ones that match the source's own published yields, so it is taken as the settlement date directly. This is an empirical finding about the source, not a convention applied to it.
- **[S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7)** settles at T+1: the download date from [2.2](#parse-market-quotes), advanced by one bond trading day using the closures from [2.4](#parse-bond-holidays).

A quote's settlement date comes from the quote file's own date. The two sources are downloaded on separate schedules, so neither file's date says anything about the other's.

**Output:** one settlement date per source.

<a id="calculate-tips-yields"></a>
### 3.1.7 Calculate the TIPS yields

**Implemented by:** `shared/src/tips-yields.js#tipsYieldsFromPrices`

Calculates the ask yield and the SA yield of each TIPS from its quoted price, the daily Ref CPI and the settlement date of the store the quote came from. The result is the input to every process downstream of 3.1 that works on TIPS, whichever store a quote came from.

One implementation serves the app and the acquisition job that writes [S13](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s13), [S14](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s14) and [S15](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s15), so the published figures and the ones the app shows are one calculation.

A security is dropped when it has no quote on the side being priced, when either [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) is unavailable for its dates, or when its ask or SA yield cannot be calculated — the same rule [3.1.2](#parse-market-quotes) applies to a nominal Treasury row.

**Nominal securities do not pass through this process.** Nominal Treasuries need no index ratio, so their security objects are assembled in two other places. The market quotes are assembled in [3.1.2](#parse-market-quotes), in `YieldCurves/src/app.js#parseFidelityNominals`, where both their yields are calculated. The [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) rows are assembled during rendering (3.7), inline in `YieldCurves/src/app.js#processAndRenderNominals`, where each yield is calculated from that file's price at its own settlement date.

**This process runs per render, not once per load.** It is called once for each source shown, each time a view is rendered, rather than once when the sources are read. The name of the parent process describes where its output belongs rather than when it is produced.

For each TIPS, the [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio) is calculated from the [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi) of its source's settlement date over its dated date Ref CPI.

For each TIPS quoted in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7), three yields are calculated from price: the ask yield, the bid yield, and the seasonally adjusted yield, the last from the ask price scaled by the [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) ratio specified in [3.2 Seasonal Adjustments](./3.2_Seasonal_Adjustments.md). No source publishes the third at all, and the first two are calculated for the reasons [3.1.2](#parse-market-quotes) gives for the nominal Treasuries in the same file.

**Output:** the TIPS yields, to [3.2 Seasonal Adjustments](./3.2_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) and [3.4 Spot Yield Curves](./3.4_Spot_Yield_Curves.md); the bid and ask quotes, to the spread comparison; and each source's date, which 3.7 shows against the view drawn from it.
