# 5.0 Load and Parse Source Data

Specifies process 7.1 of the Yield Curves data flow diagram and the seven processes it decomposes into.

## Dependencies

- [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) — [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield), [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price), [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date), [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio), [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor), [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi)
- [DataStores.md](../../knowledge/DataStores.md) — [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1), [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4), [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7), [S12](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s12), and the bond holidays file
- [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) and [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md) — the processes downstream of this one

---

## 1.0 Scope

Yield Curves reads five data stores and produces one set of securities that the rest of the app works from. Every process below runs in the browser at page load, and none of them writes a data store.

**Yields are not calculated here for nominal securities.** [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) already carries a [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield) per security, calculated from price when the file was written by ingestion process 1.1, and [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) carries the yield its source quotes. This stage reads those. The only yields calculated here are the seasonally adjusted ones, in [2.7](#build-priced-bonds), because no source publishes them.

## 2.0 Processes

<a id="parse-fedinvest-prices"></a>
### 2.1 Parse FedInvest prices (7.1.1)

Reads [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) and splits it into the TIPS and the nominal securities, by its `Type` column. That split is what lets one store supply both the real curve and the nominal curve.

[S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1) states one [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) for the whole file rather than one per security, so that date is read once and attached to every security taken from it. See [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates) for what that date means for this source.

**Output:** the securities in [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1), each with its [Clean Price](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#clean-price), its [Yield](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#yield), and that settlement date.

<a id="parse-market-quotes"></a>
### 2.2 Parse market quotes (7.1.2)

Reads [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) and separates its Treasury rows from its TIPS rows, by its `Product` column, taking the bid and ask price and the ask yield for each.

The column names are the export's own rather than the Data Dictionary's. They are recorded as synonyms in the [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) entry, which gives the defined term each one carries.

[S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) carries the date it was downloaded on a trailing line rather than in a column. That date is read here and passed to [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates), which is the only thing that uses it.

**Output:** the securities in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) with their bid and ask prices and ask yields, and the date the file was downloaded.

<a id="parse-ref-cpi-and-sa-factors"></a>
### 2.3 Parse Ref CPI and SA factors (7.1.3)

Reads [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4), giving one [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi) and one [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) per calendar day.

Lookup is by exact date. [S4](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s4) carries a row for every calendar day it covers, so a date inside its range always matches exactly and a date outside it has no value at all. Nothing falls back to a nearby date.

**Output:** the daily Ref CPI series, and the SA factors that [2.7](#build-priced-bonds) and [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) apply.

<a id="parse-bond-holidays"></a>
### 2.4 Parse bond holidays (7.1.4)

Reads the bond holidays file and produces the set of dates the bond market is closed. [2.6](#determine-settlement-dates) is the only process that uses it, and the only reason this store is read.

**Output:** the bond market closure dates.

<a id="parse-gsw-parameters"></a>
### 2.5 Parse GSW parameters (7.1.5)

Reads [S12](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s12), which holds the Federal Reserve's own published curve parameters and the date they were fitted for.

Nothing is evaluated here. [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md) evaluates them, to draw the published curve against the app's own fit.

**Output:** the published curve parameters and their observation date.

<a id="determine-settlement-dates"></a>
### 2.6 Determine settlement dates (7.1.6)

Produces the [Settlement Date](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#settlement-date) each source's prices are stated at. The two sources differ, and neither is derived from the other.

- **[S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1)** carries a single date and says nothing about what it means. Yields calculated at that date, rather than at a date offset from it, are the ones that match the source's own published yields, so it is taken as the settlement date directly. This is an empirical finding about the source, not a convention applied to it.
- **[S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7)** settles at T+1: the download date from [2.2](#parse-market-quotes), advanced by one bond trading day using the closures from [2.4](#parse-bond-holidays).

A quote's settlement date comes from the quote file's own date. The two sources are downloaded on separate schedules, so neither file's date says anything about the other's.

**Output:** one settlement date per source.

<a id="build-priced-bonds"></a>
### 2.7 Build the security set (7.1.7)

Combines the parsed securities, the daily Ref CPI and the settlement dates into a single consolidated set, which is the input to every process downstream of 7.1 regardless of which store a security came from.

For each TIPS, the [Index Ratio](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#index-ratio) is calculated from the [Ref CPI](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#ref-cpi) of its source's settlement date over its dated date Ref CPI.

For each TIPS quoted in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7), three yields are calculated from price, because no source publishes them: the ask yield, the bid yield, and the seasonally adjusted yield, the last from the ask price scaled by the [SA Factor](../../knowledge/DATA_DICTIONARY.md#sa-factor) ratio specified in [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md). Calculating the bid yield from price rather than reading the quoted bid yield keeps it on the same basis as the ask yield, so the spread between them is a spread and not an artefact of two conventions.

**Output:** the security set, to [1.0 Seasonal Adjustments](./1.0_Seasonal_Adjustments.md) and [4.0 Spot Yield Curves](./4.0_Spot_Yield_Curves.md); the bid and ask quotes, to the spread comparison; and each source's date, which 7.7 shows against the view drawn from it.

## 3.0 Known Defect

[2.6](#determine-settlement-dates) does not behave as specified. The settlement date for TIPS quoted in [S7](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s7) is currently taken from [S1](../../knowledge/DataStores.md#s1)'s date instead of from the quote file's own date. The correct derivation already exists in the app and is used for the nominal fit, the breakeven fit and the source labels; the TIPS path has not been moved onto it.

This changes the seasonally adjusted and breakeven figures for TIPS on any day the two sources were downloaded on different dates, which is most days.
